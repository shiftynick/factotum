# Operational controls

The ordinary modules in `starter/factory/operations/index.mjs` use the project Store's existing records and metadata. They introduce no database schema. Time-taking functions accept `nowMs` for repeatable failure exercises. A caller must never invoke an operation that owns a transaction inside another Store transaction; the explicitly documented callbacks are the exception and must perform only synchronous durable writes, never network or process effects.

## Admission

`admissionReasons(store, task, {nowMs, policy=store.meta('config',{}).policy??{}})` returns reasons that prevent admission. Policy accepts `maxFlows` (default 1), `mainHealthMaxAgeMs` (default one hour), `categories`, `requirePaths`, and `recovery`. Tasks need current `approvedRevision===revision`, must remain open and unblocked, and can specify `dependencies`, `area`, `paths`, and `recovery`. Dependencies require the referenced task's `status` to be `completed`. Active ownership excludes only completed and failed flows; stopped flows retain ownership until explicit disposition. Areas and overlapping path prefixes exclude concurrent work. A required path plan can be enabled before concurrent operation.

### Flow slots and parked flows

`policy.maxFlows` (default 1) and `policy.maxDocumentFlows` (default 2) bound the change and document flows that hold a slot (`operations/capacity.mjs`). A flow holds one from admission until it finishes, except while it is parked.

- **Parked.** A flow waiting at an open question, with nothing running, holds no slot: it does nothing until someone answers. (Between reaching the question and the engine opening it, a tick later, it still holds its slot, so a merge question that `policy.autoMerge` answers as it opens never gives its slot away.) Every question node parks: a plan, build, conflict, review or gate question, the merge decision (including a strict sign-off), the satisfied-outcome decision and any question in a project-owned graph. Waiting on anything else (a timer, a target wait, a provider usage-limit hold, a decision hold) is not parked and keeps the slot. Parking is read from the flow's step, not stored, so a flow that was already waiting on an answer before an upgrade is parked at once.
- **The parked limit.** At most `policy.maxParkedFlows` (default 3, live, 0–50) change and document flows together are parked without a slot, the longest-parked first. A flow parked beyond that holds a slot in its own pool again, and admission says so: `flow capacity exhausted; parked flows beyond policy.maxParkedFlows (3) hold slots: <flow ids> (answer their questions)`. So at most `maxFlows + maxParkedFlows` change flows are open at once. `0` turns parking off: every waiting flow holds its slot, as before dev.83.
- **Answered.** An answer (or `flow.replan`) that sends a parked flow back to work takes a slot if its pool has one. If not, the flow waits: `state: waiting`, `slotWait {since, step, after}`, event `flow.slot-wait`. It is not stopped and needs no resume. The engine gives it the first free slot (`flow.slot-granted`), ahead of every queued task; while a flow waits, admission reports `flow capacity exhausted; answered flows take the next free slot first: <flow ids>`, naming every answered flow ahead of the queued task in line order, also while working flows hold all the slots (from dev.84; before, only the flows a slot was free for now), and leaving out a waiting flow whose own category is full, which is passed over until its category frees. The console renders each named flow as a link. The line is: answered merges first (a flow at its merge step, `slotWait.merge`; a merge finishes work and frees its worktree and paths), then other answered flows, each group in the order the waits began, then queued tasks. An answer never starts more flows than there are slots. An answer that leads to another question or to an end node takes no slot. `flow.replan` follows the same rule: only a flow at a working step that is neither stopped nor waiting for a slot continues at once. Every other flow joins the line in order (a parked or waiting flow; a stopped flow; a flow at a question or end node in any other state, such as one answered `stop` a moment ago or resumed and not asked again yet; a finished flow): it continues at once only if its pool has room for it after every flow already waiting, and otherwise waits behind them. Unlike a resumed flow, a replanned stopped flow does not keep its slot ahead of flows that are waiting. A finished (abandoned or failed) flow is brought back only if nothing admitted since excludes it: its task has no other open flow and its area and paths are free.
- **Stopped.** A stopped flow holds a slot, including one stopped while it was parked. In particular a question that expires unanswered (after 24 hours unless the node sets `expiryMs`) stops its flow, and from then on that flow holds a slot until it is resumed or abandoned: an expired question takes back the slot that the open question had freed. `resume` continues it at once, unless it had no slot when it was stopped and its pool is now full; then it waits for one like an answered flow.
- **Categories.** `policy.categoryLimits` follows the same rules, counting places in the category instead of slots: a parked flow within the parked limit holds no place in its category, so a category limited to 1 is not held by an open question; a working flow, a stopped flow and a flow parked beyond the limit hold one. An answered flow needs a place in its category as well as a slot and waits for whichever is missing (`category capacity exhausted: <name>; answered flows take the next free place first: <flow ids>` for queued tasks meanwhile); a waiting flow whose category is full does not hold up waiting flows of other categories, and reserves no pool slot: a slot (or a category place) is kept from queued tasks only for a waiting flow that can take it now. The waiting flow keeps its place in the line for when its category frees. While a slot stands free for that reason, `queueStatus.capacity.categoryWaits` (and the idle-capacity notice, and the console's Flow slots card) names the waiting flow, the category and the flows that hold it.
- **Everything else still applies to parked flows.** A parked flow's declared paths, plan files and candidate changes exclude overlapping work, its area stays occupied, its task cannot be started again, and it blocks a configuration update, doctor and an upgrade like any unfinished change flow (its worktree and pinned executor are still in use). Merge holds and main health apply as before. A flow that was answered `merge` but is waiting for a slot is not a merge in flight: nothing waits on it.
- **Task flows** are unchanged: `policy.maxTaskFlows` counts every open task flow.

Where it shows: `queueStatus.capacity` in `status`, `status --compact` and the console's Queue tab (per pool, and per category with a limit: slots in use, free, parked, parked beyond the limit, waiting; each parked flow with its step and whether it holds a slot; each waiting flow with its reason and position). `status --flow` adds `flow.parked`, `flow.slotWait {position, reason}` and `project.capacity`. Flow lists carry `parked`. The idle-capacity notice counts a slot a parked flow freed, and not one an answered flow is waiting for.

A flow's own changes, for the path check, are what its worktree differs in from the target it last integrated (`results.integrate.base`, when the candidate contains it or is merging it). Before an integration, or when the candidate does not contain the recorded one, they are compared with the worktree's original base, which can only list more. So a flow that has integrated a moved target no longer excludes tasks that touch what other work merged.

A merge in flight holds admission into its target: while another change flow for the same target is at its `forge.merge` step (answered `merge`, preparing or running, and not stopped), admission reports `<target> merge in progress (flow …); new work waits for it and its post-merge verification` and the Queue tab shows a `merge` hold. A flow already admitted that reaches its source step meanwhile waits there (`state: waiting`, `targetWait`, events `flow.target-wait` and `flow.target-released`) instead of stopping, while the merge is in flight or the target's health is not current and a `main.verify` for it is queued or running; it continues by itself once the target is verified green. When the source check finds the target at a head nobody verified and nothing is verifying it (someone pushed to the target just before the flow started), the flow asks for that verification itself and waits for it the same way: one `main.verify` under the `policy.autoQueue` grant, events `flow.verification-requested` and `main.verify-requested {cause}`. It asks only where queued work could: `policy.autoQueue` on, doctor passed for the current configuration, admission not paused or held, the target not red, and no verification of it failed in the last 15 minutes; and at most three times per source visit. Otherwise it stops at source and the reason names the cause and what to run, for example `main is not verified for source admission and no verification was requested: automatic verification is off (policy.autoQueue). To continue: run main.verify for main (job.enqueue {operation: "main.verify"}) or doctor; wait for current green verification, then resume this flow.` If the target was verified green while the source check ran (the check judged an older record), the check is simply repeated, counted with those three (`flow.source-recheck`). A red verification or a pending or held target synchronization still stops it at source as before. "In flight" and "on its way" are judged from the flow doing the work, not from a job's status: a merge flow that is stopped, asked to stop, held by a decision or has an uncertain attempt holds nothing, and a `main.verify` job whose verifier flow is stopped (or missing, finished, held or uncertain), or that is queued behind a stopped maintenance flow or a maintenance hold, cannot proceed. Then the source flow stops (`flow.target-wait-blocked`) with a reason naming the job and verifier and the recovery: resume the verifier or abandon it and run `main.verify` again (or doctor), then resume the source flow, which waits for the verification and continues when the target is green.

### A target that moves while a merge question is open

An open merge question states when the target's recorded head (target health: a verification, another flow's merge, or the target probe) is no longer what the candidate integrated: `targetMoved {target, from, to, changed, overlap, overlapCount, observedAt}` on the item and a paragraph in its `context`: "The target main moved from X to Y after this candidate was integrated at X", followed by what answering `merge` then does. The first move raises the item's version, so an answer given on the earlier card is refused as stale and the notification consumer is told once; later moves update the same card. The comparison is three local Git reads when the recorded head or the integrated base changes, none per tick and none over the network; a move whose commits were not in the local repository yet is compared again, no more often than the question is revalidated, once a fetch has brought them. A recorded head *behind* the integrated base is a move back (a force-push or reset) when it was observed after the question opened by the probe or a verification, and is ignored when the record is merely older than the integration. A pending presentation of the question on a prompt channel ends as `obsolete` when the version rises, and its presenter is told, so that no answer is relayed for the earlier card. So that a push made outside the factory is noticed, the target probe also runs for a target with an open merge question, with or without `policy.autoQueue` and without queued work: one `git ls-remote` per target at most every five minutes (`MERGE_QUESTION_PROBE_MS`; `policy.mainProbeMs` when that is longer), only while such a question is open, never per tick, and also while admission is paused or under a recovery hold; `policy.mainProbeMs: 0` turns it off. Whether a read is due is checked before the queue or any flow is read. Five minutes because a merge question waits on a person for minutes to hours and the merge step reads the target itself whatever the card says. A move it finds makes the target's health unknown (`main.moved`), as for queued work; without `policy.autoQueue` nothing verifies it automatically, so admission then waits for `main.verify`, and a further move is noticed once the target is verified again.

The merge step decides with the same rule (`operations/target-move.mjs`). Before merging it reads the target's current head from the remote (the forge's pull request `base` only says what the pull request was last synchronized with). Only the line for exactly `refs/heads/<target>` counts (a branch named `x/refs/heads/<target>` is listed by the same `git ls-remote`). The read and the fetch after it are each bounded at 30 seconds (Git and its descendants are terminated; Git stays in the attempt's process group, so stopping the attempt ends it too). They end differently. If the head cannot be read (`ls-remote` fails or times out), the target counts as unreadable. If the head was read and differs but its commits cannot be fetched (the fetch fails or times out), the move is known and what it touches is not, so the step returns `stale` and the flow re-integrates. A target found behind the integrated base (moved back) goes back to integration like an overlapping move. If the target moved and its new commits change a file the candidate changed since integration, or a path the task or reviewed plan declares, or that cannot be told, the step returns `stale` and the flow goes back to integration: checks and review run again on the re-integrated candidate and a new merge question follows. If the new commits touch nothing of the candidate, the merge goes ahead onto the moved target without integrating again, and the move is recorded on the merge result (`results.merge.targetMoved`). The card says so plainly beforehand: the review and the checks ran against the earlier target, they are not repeated, and only post-merge verification checks the combined result. A change elsewhere in the target can still break the candidate (a renamed function it calls, say); that is found after the merge, not before. If the target's head cannot be read, the merge proceeds as before dev.83.

**Merges into one target run one at a time** (dev.84). Two merges answered together used to run at once: the forge merged the first and refused the second as out of date, which sent it back through integration, a full review and the merge question. Now a flow's merge step waits while another flow's merge into the same target is running (its `forge.merge` attempt is prepared, dispatched or running) or has merged and is at its post-merge step: `state: waiting`, `mergeWait {target, flowIds, reason, since}` (shown by `status --flow` and the narrow reads), events `flow.merge-wait` and `flow.merge-released`. The reason reads, for example, "Waiting: flow F is merging into main. Merges into one target run one at a time; this merge starts when that has finished, then reads main again: …". When the first has finished, the waiting merge step runs as above: it reads the moved target and merges without a new review when the new commits do not touch its candidate, and goes back to integration when they do. Only progressing flows count: a merge flow that is stopped, asked to stop or has an uncertain merge attempt holds no other merge (the next merge reads the target itself), and a flow waiting here has no attempt, so two waits never hold each other. Merges into different targets do not wait for each other. A waiting merge keeps its flow slot, and it counts as a merge in flight for admission. If the forge itself refuses the merge because the branch is out of date with its base (branch protection that requires an up-to-date branch; recognised from the refusal's text or from the pull request reading `mergeable_state: behind`), the step also returns `stale` and the flow re-integrates, instead of failing. A race between this read and the forge's merge remains unless remote branch protection enforces freshness.

Main health lives at `meta('mainHealth')` as `{status,checkedAt,head}`. Unknown, red, future-dated or stale observations hold admission. When some `mainChecks` have `tier: "scheduled"`, red, missing or expired scheduled-tier evidence holds admission too; see [main-check tiers](#main-check-tiers-after-every-merge-or-scheduled-only). The only health bypass requires both an explicit recovery admission policy and a task marked `recovery:true`; project pause and recovery holds still apply. The GitHub integration must verify the observed head against current main before recording green health. The helper alone does not contact the forge.

`claimCandidate(store,{taskId?,policy?,nowMs?,admit})` opens one transaction, selects the first eligible queued task if no task ID is supplied, calls `admit(task,store)`, and marks its `queue` record claimed in the same commit. Queue IDs are task IDs; records have `status:'queued'`. The callback must synchronously persist and return the new flow and must not call the transaction-owning canonical command executor. Throwing rolls back both changes. No eligible task returns null. An explicit ineligible task throws.

### Target synchronization and source holds

A queued or running `target.sync` holds task admission into its destination, including recovery tasks. Dispatch sets that destination's health to unknown before the worker can push. Settlement invalidates it again, so a verifier started before or during the sync cannot leave stale green behind. Even a no-op or failed sync requires a fresh target observation. Other branches retain their own health. With `autoQueue` enabled, waiting queued work requests the normal `main.verify` job for its target; otherwise authorize that job explicitly with `payload: {target: "next"}` (using the project's actual branch).

From dev.75, dispatch also records a durable synchronization hold for the configured `{from, into}` pair. A failed, interrupted or unresolved sync keeps this hold even if destination verification later passes. The queue shows the reason; acknowledging its notice does not release it, and recovery tasks cannot bypass it. A clean successful sync, including a no-op that proves the fetched source head is already an ancestor of the destination, clears only its own pair's hold. Fresh destination verification is still required afterward. Removing a pair through reviewed, commissioned configuration ends that pair's admission requirement.

After manually resolving and pushing a conflict, explicitly enqueue `target.sync` with `payload: {from: "main", into: "next"}` for the configured branches, then obtain current destination verification. Acknowledgment or a green health override is not proof of integration. Generated `target.sync` and `main.verify` workflows now settle failed routes as failed jobs; sync failure notices carry the retained reason. Historical completed jobs and acknowledged notices are not rewritten, and no holds are retroactively inferred from old failures. When upgrading with an unresolved historical failure, keep admission paused or automatic dispatch disabled until the explicit sync and verification have succeeded.

An already-admitted flow that encounters a synchronization hold at its gate stops there without routing to the builder. Project checks can also declare reserved prerequisite exit codes through [`checks[].holdExitCodes`](project-workflows.md#gate-prerequisites-that-builders-cannot-repair). Such a stop retains its queue claim and flow capacity. Inspect the evidence and repair the prerequisite. If the candidate needs branch integration, explicitly restart from the repaired target after preserving any needed work; a plain resume repeats the gate and does not integrate branches. Existing review and exact-candidate authority checks remain enforced.

Each canonical health change advances an internal per-target generation. A verifier can publish health only if its dispatch generation is still current; a failed attempt cannot publish green. Superseded results remain in attempt history and emit `main.verification-superseded`. Existing stores start at generation zero; no schema migration is required.

Source verification also compares the observed remote head and fetched head with its health snapshot. A mismatch produces a typed hold before workspace creation. Settlement invalidates the matching stale health and stops the flow at source without finishing its queue entry. A newer health observation is preserved. Inspect the retained source result, obtain current target verification, then explicitly `resume` the same flow. Resume still requires valid approval; a stop or revocation made while the check ran is preserved. An attempt prepared before a health change or pending sync is stopped before it can dispatch.

For source-only terminal failures from an earlier release, retain their evidence and explicitly requeue with current approval, preserving the intended order. First check that each task has no active or unsettled flow. Pausing admission does not abort attempts already running, and acknowledgment of a notice does not release flow ownership. Project pause prevents new maintenance jobs from starting too. If no verification is already running, disable automatic queue dispatch (and any scheduled/manual `queue.dispatch`), release the pause to run an explicitly authorized target verification, and restore dispatch only after green matches the current remote head. Do not fabricate a green health exception to clear the backlog. Normal release adoption still requires a drained runtime.

## Durable schedules

`dueOccurrences(schedule,{nowMs,afterMs,maxCatchUp=8,windowMs=86400000})` computes occurrences without effects. An interval schedule uses `{id,type:'interval',everyMs,anchorMs}`; a daily schedule uses `{id,type:'daily',timeOfDay:'09:00',timezone:'America/New_York',weekdays?:[1,2,3,4,5]}`. Weekdays use Sunday=0. The window is exclusive at `afterMs` and inclusive at `nowMs`; catch-up is oldest first, bounded to at most 1000 occurrences and a seven-day window. Daily times have minute resolution. A nonexistent time during the spring DST transition is skipped; the repeated fall time yields one occurrence per local calendar day. Change a schedule's ID to establish a new occurrence namespace when changing its semantics.

`dispatchSchedules(store,{nowMs,dispatch,maxCatchUp,windowMs})` reads enabled `schedule` records with `createdAt`, persists `occurrence` claims, and calls `dispatch(schedule,occurrence,store)` in the same transaction. Return `{commandId}` for the receipt link. This callback must create a durable command/flow, never perform an external effect. A failure rolls back the occurrence, cursor, and callback writes. Existing occurrences suppress duplicate triggers after restarts. A bounded backlog continues next tick. Time outside the catch-up window is skipped with a `schedule.missed` event. This is the only timer owner; a host integration should deliver wakeups rather than create another scheduler.

## Machine resources

`new HostResources(sharedSqlitePath,{inspectOwner?})` opens a separate host-wide SQLite coordinator shared by every project. `acquire({resource='heavy-work',projectId,flowId,attemptId,owner})` returns a lease with an opaque `token`, or null when occupied. `release(resource,token)` rejects a mismatched owner token. `reconcile(resource)` returns absent/alive/dead/uncertain and only removes proven dead owners. `close()` releases the connection.

The owner is `{pid,bootId,startTime,cgroup?}` from the Linux process identity helper, and must identify the supervisor that lives for the whole operation. Using the shorter-lived runner would permit premature reclamation. When a validated `/sys/fs/cgroup/...` path is supplied, the owner remains alive while any process exists in its cgroup or nested cgroups, even after its original supervisor exits; failed inspection is uncertain. A removed cgroup or fully inspected empty cgroup proves the contained operation is dead. Real heavy operations should use this contained owner identity. Without a cgroup, a changed boot/start identity or a missing proc entry proves death; permission and I/O errors are uncertain and retain the lease. A successful repeated acquire with exactly the same identity returns the existing lease. Heavy action workers retain their lease after returning; the next contender may reclaim it only after the supervisor cgroup has fully drained. A worker must never release the supervisor lease merely because its own callback finished. Standalone commissioning holds a process-owned lease until its bounded subprocess groups are drained, then releases it. Each resource is exclusive; this first implementation deliberately has no weighted or shared leases. Resource names used by different projects must match to coordinate. All projects using a shared file must trust its owner; it is not a cross-user isolation boundary.

## Money

`reserveMoney(store,{id,flowId,ceilingMicros,capMicros,capability})` requires safe nonnegative integer micro-units. Paid execution requires `capability.enforceableCeiling===true`, an assertion that the specific adapter actually enforces the provider request's limit. This flag is not itself enforcement. Without such an adapter only zero-cost subscription operations are supported. Actual spend plus all reserved or unknown ceilings must fit the cap. Reusing an ID with different reservation input is rejected.

`settleMoney(store,id,actualMicros)` records known actual spend, releasing the reservation; null marks unknown and retains its entire reservation. Repeated equal settlement is idempotent; conflicting settlement is rejected. An actual amount above its ceiling is retained in full and sets both project pause and a monetary hold. A monetary hold blocks further reservations until explicit operator reconciliation; clearing the ordinary pause alone does not clear it. These helpers never invoke paid clients or estimate costs from historical replay.

## Governing context

`assembleContext({repoRoot,config:{files,maxChars},changedPaths=[]})` returns complete file excerpts, content digests, optional omission reasons, and a package digest. Entries are `{id,path,required,paths?}`; `paths` is an optional wildcard mapping against changed paths. Required mapped entries must match, be readable, remain within the real project root, and fit the budget; otherwise assembly fails closed. Optional excerpts can be omitted with an explicit reason. Required excerpts take priority, and no excerpt is truncated. Patterns support `*` as any sequence of characters; these are mapping patterns, not filesystem globs. Context files are explicit project configuration, never hidden user memory.

`contextDelivery(store,{consumerId,context})` prepares excerpts whose ID/content-digest pair differs from the last acknowledgment. `acknowledgeContext(store,{consumerId,context})` is called only after confirmed delivery. The same ID with changed contents therefore delivers again. Preparing a package does not falsely mark it received.

## Recovery hold and operations faults

A recovery hold (`meta('recoveryHold')`) stops admission and dispatch until the operator reconciles state and releases it. It is placed only when the integrity of the state is in doubt, and `meta('recoveryHoldReason')` records why as `{cause, error?, at}`:

- `restore`: the state was restored from a backup (`restore` also pauses admission).
- `runner-fault`: the flow runner's tick failed as a whole (event `runner.fault`).
- `operations-fault`: the operations loop hit a database fault that leaves the state in doubt: an I/O error, corruption, a full disk, or a read-only, unopenable or non-database file (SQLite primary codes 8, 10, 11, 13, 14 and 26). Event `operations.fault {error, recoveryHold: true}`.

Every other exception in the operations loop (a failing optional integration, a scheduled operation that cannot be dispatched, a programming error) leaves no partial state, because its transaction is rolled back. It is recorded once as `meta('operationsFault') = {error, since, changedAt}` with the event `operations.fault {error, recoveryHold: false}` (again only when the error changes), shown under `health.operationsFault` in `status` and as "Operations loop fault" in the console, and cleared by the next tick that completes (event `operations.recovered`). The loop keeps running, and running flows are not stopped.

A flow that meets a hold is stopped with a reason that says what happened, for example `recovery hold: the operations loop hit a state-integrity fault (disk I/O error); reconcile unsettled attempts, stop active flows and release it with restore.release`. A hold placed by an earlier release has no recorded cause and says so.

To recover from a real hold:

1. Find the cause: `status` shows `recoveryHoldReason`; the events above carry the error. For a database fault, stop the project service, check the disk and run `sqlite3 <state>/state.sqlite 'PRAGMA integrity_check'`. If the database is damaged, restore the latest backup into a new state directory (`restore --from … --state …`) instead of continuing.
2. Reconcile every unsettled attempt: `reconcile` for typed external effects, or `attempt.resolve` after inspecting its evidence.
3. Stop any active flow (`stop`).
4. Release: `command --operation restore.release --payload '{"note":"…"}' --actor operator`. The event `restore.reconciled` records the note and the cause. Pause and stopped flows are left as they are; `resume` the flows you want to continue.

## Notifications

`queueDelivery(store,{inboxId,channel='operator',nowMs})` creates a stable record per inbox ID, inbox version, and channel. `deliverDue(store,{configPath,transport,nowMs,limit=8,timeoutMs=10000})` is asynchronous and requires an explicitly injected transport. The private configuration must be a regular 0600 file containing an HTTPS `url` and optional `signingKey`; neither is copied into Store records, events, or error text. The transport receives `{url,payload,headers,signal}` and returns `{status}`. Payload contains project/inbox/delivery IDs, the inbox version, `observedAt`, `status`, and `event` (`inbox.attention` or `inbox.resolved`). Answers remain in canonical state, outside the webhook payload. Optional signatures are HMAC-SHA256 in `x-factotum-signature` over the exact serialized payload. The receiver must verify the signature and deduplicate delivery IDs.

**Configuration problems pause delivery; they never stop work.** Notification delivery is an optional integration. When the configuration file at `notifications.configPath` is missing (including a typo in the path), not a private 0600 file, not valid JSON, or has no valid HTTPS `url`, `deliverDue` sends nothing and records the problem once: `meta('notificationHealth')` becomes `{status: "paused", problem, since, changedAt}` and the event `notification.paused {problem}` is written only when the problem changes. The problem text names neither the path nor the file's contents. Deliveries stay queued. `status` shows it under `health.notifications`, the console Overview shows "Notification delivery paused", and `doctor` lists it as a warning, not a reason. The first tick that reads a valid file clears it (event `notification.resumed {problem, pausedSince}`) and sends what is queued, minus deliveries whose item changed meanwhile, which are superseded as usual. Admission, dispatch and running flows are unaffected, and there is no recovery hold.

**Delivery starting: no backlog.** Deliveries are queued for inbox changes even before a consumer is configured. When a configuration update starts delivery (sets `notifications.configPath` on a delivery project where it was unset, switches to delivery mode with one, or changes it to another file), only attention deliveries for items that are still open, and wakes still owed to a presenter, are kept. Everything else queued (resolutions of settled items, attention for items no longer open, state events) is marked `superseded` with `supersededReason: "backlog before notification delivery started"`. The event `notification.backlog-trimmed {kept, superseded, supersededByKind}` and `config.update`'s result field `notificationBacklog` summarize it; settled history stays readable in the inbox and History. A delivery paused by a file problem is not trimmed when it resumes.

Network attempts are durably claimed before sending and use an expiring send lease, allowing recovery after process death. Non-2xx outcomes retry with exponential delay from 30 seconds to four hours. A crash after remote acceptance can produce a duplicate delivery with the same ID, so receiver deduplication is required. A transport must honor its abort signal; the helper cannot terminate an arbitrary uncooperative injected function. Missing items and deliveries whose version or status no longer matches are superseded before dispatch. Maintenance creates a new resolution delivery for terminal dispositions, including expired items.

A 2xx result marks transport acceptance only. The canonical consumer command is `delivery.ack` with payload `{ "deliveryId": "..." }`; `acknowledgeDelivery(store,{deliveryId,nowMs})` is the internal helper. Neither operation answers a canonical inbox question or authorizes an effect. Canonical answer commands still check actor, version, subject digest, and current authority. Reminder policy can enqueue a new version/channel delivery explicitly; accepted deliveries remind every 30 minutes while the canonical item remains open; acknowledgment defers the next reminder to at least four hours out and never brings it earlier: an item whose own interval already put the next reminder later (for example 24 hours) keeps it there. Before dev.82 an acknowledgment always set it four hours out, which for such an item was sooner. Resolution, expiration, or version changes suppress old deliveries and produce a separate resolution wake. Terminal deliveries retry until `delivery.ack` records consumption; they never re-open or answer an inbox item.

**Reminders.** Open questions are re-sent every 30 minutes after an accepted delivery until they are resolved; a `delivery.ack` moves the next one to four hours out, or leaves it where it was if that is later. Open notices are delivered once by default and then marked `delivered`; they are not re-sent. A standalone publisher can override this per item with `remind` on `inbox.publish`: `"never"`, or an interval in milliseconds from 30 minutes to 7 days. Items that set their own reminder interval internally, such as the idle-queue notice, keep it.

**Prompt presentations.** While an [external prompt channel is showing a question](communication.md#prompt-presentations-and-relayed-answers), its reminders are held until that presentation ends; the first delivery, retries and resolutions are sent as usual. When a presentation expires, or ends through someone other than its presenter, the item's current delivery is sent once more at every notification level, including for an item the orchestrator published or a superseded resolution, so the presenter can take the prompt down. A delivery that was `delivered` or `suppressed` before that wake returns to that status afterwards. Its payload says so: `presentationEnded: true` and the ended presentation's `presentationId` (absent on every other wake). The wake is done only when a request dispatched after the presentation ended is accepted: a request already in flight when it ended does not count, so the delivery is sent once more after it; a failed request is retried; an acknowledgement neither settles nor defers it. Presentations that end again before that request is accepted share the one wake and keep the status the delivery had before the first. If the question is resolved while a wake is still owed, the wake moves to the resolution's delivery.

**Notification level.** `notifications.level` is `essential` by default:

- The consumer is woken for inbox questions and notices, for approval changes (`task.approved`, `task.revoked`, `task.approval-revoked`) and for one notice per flow end.
- Nothing the orchestrator caused itself wakes it. That covers its own commands' state events, the flow-end notice and resolutions that follow from them, and the items it published.
- Flow admission, resume and end state events, and superseded resolutions, never wake.

Payloads carry no prose and no answers. A consumer reads the item itself: a plan question's item has the planner's questions in `context` and `planQuestion`, and its answers' labels in `choices` (`replan` "Replan with my answers", `proceed` "Proceed with this plan" when offered, `stop`). See [plan questions](project-workflows.md#plan-questions).
- `all` restores every event, with flow ends arriving both as state events and as notices; `suppressSelfResolutions` still applies there.

**Quieter terminal summaries.** Set `notifications.level: "actionable"` to use essential routing while suppressing proven clean delivery and `target.sync` terminal summary deliveries, including their later acknowledgment resolutions. Questions, substantive operator answers, approval/revocation changes, failures, stops, uncertain outcomes, source changes/partial results, findings and outstanding obligations remain visible. Routine green built-in `main.verify` was already normally quiet. Core flow summaries remain in the inbox and History; delivery filtering does not resolve them or remove evidence. Unknown/custom success and missing cleanup evidence stay visible. Existing essential/all behavior and the default are unchanged.

On switching to `actionable`, pending deliveries, expired send leases and accepted retries are rechecked against current policy at dispatch. Ineligible rows become `suppressed`; actionable attention remains pending (already accepted non-reminding notices remain delivered once). Active sends cannot be recalled. Existing suppressed rows are not replayed on switching back. See [the exact semantics and compatibility policy](changes/plan-01-quiet-notifications.md).

**Orchestrator session.** Every payload also carries `sessionVersion`, `sessionDeliveries` and `sessionDeliveryLimit`: the session the number belongs to, this delivery's send number in the current orchestrator session (unique per delivery, kept by retries) and the handoff limit; the handoff question counts accepted deliveries. At `notifications.handoffAfterDeliveries` (default 100; 0 turns the question off) Factotum raises one handoff question to the orchestrator, re-raised only after `notifications.handoffRepeatDeliveries` more (default 25). Suppressed deliveries are not counted. The question never holds admission or delivery. See [orchestrator session and handoff](communication.md#orchestrator-session-and-handoff).

**Resolution wakes.** `inbox.resolved` payloads include `resolution` (`answered`, `acknowledged`, `withdrawn`, `expired` or `superseded`) and `actor` when it is known. This is attribution only; consumers still read canonical state. With `notifications.suppressSelfResolutions: true`, resolutions made by the `orchestrator` role, which consumes the webhook, are recorded as `suppressed` deliveries and not sent.

## Feedback, retention, upgrades

`metrics(store)` reports observed flow states, attempt counts, duration samples from created/finished timestamps, explicit `wait` spans, actual monetary records, and unknown counts. Empty timing samples have a null mean. No wall-clock remainder is guessed to be productive time or wait. `retrospective(store,{flowId})` projects stored attempts, pushback, and the latest 10000 events, explicitly reporting its bounded history limitation.

`auditFindings(store,{findings,limit=8,source='audit'})` caps findings and deduplicates title/evidence/source digests. Findings are always unapproved and never directly launch work. `recordPushback(store,{ruleId,flowId,reason,evidence})` records a proposed rule change with evidence; it never changes policy automatically.

`retentionPlan(store,{artifactRoot,candidates,preservation})` is a dry run. The injected preservation hook must return `{complete:true,protectedPaths:[...],...evidence}`. Active flows, unresolved attempts or open inbox items stop deletion entirely. The entire canonical state root, including settled attempt evidence, is always protected. Existing file/directory references found in durable records protect external paths as well. Referenced, preserved, and flow/attempt-associated artifact records are protected. Unresolved obligations and stopped flows hold deletion. A project preservation hook can add protections, never override canonical references. Failed preservation, unsafe paths, symlinks or unsupported files result in a skipped plan. Only ordinary files are supported. `applyRetention(store,{manifest,approvedDigest,preservation})` requires the explicitly approved plan digest, repeats preservation and state checks, verifies file digests, then deletes only listed files and emits deletion evidence. Retention must run with exclusive maintenance ownership so another process cannot create a new artifact reference between verification and unlink. A partially executed manifest is not silently resumed: create and approve a fresh plan for surviving files.

**Upgrade apply and the project's state (dev.87).** Two records in the state directory, outside the checkout, back `upgrade apply`:

- `upgradeRecovery`, from just before `--merge` or `--activate` stops the first unit until every unit it stopped is confirmed running again: the stopped units, and the commit the checkout held, which only shapes the instructions and is never trusted. Units are stopped only on a checkout that verifies from its files, and started again only when it does with an authenticated lock (doctor's drift check: the lock and its evidence, the anchor, each installed file hashed from its bytes; a root lock without evidence needs `--recover --release`), and in a recovery only after `config update --current` and doctor have passed; otherwise they stay stopped and the result's `hold` or `units` step ends with the exact commands to run. Nothing is stashed: the instructions ask for anything worth keeping to be copied outside the repository. `upgrade apply --recover`, or the next `--merge` or `--activate`, finishes an unfinished run first (a `recover` step), and preparing an upgrade is refused while the record is there. See [updates](updates.md#install-under-a-folder), "When an upgrade stops partway".
- `adoptionAnchor`, from the first time the project's lock verifies by its layout evidence: after that, doctor (a blocking reason), `adopt status --state`, `--merge` and `--activate` refuse a root lock without evidence unless a verified release directory authenticated that exact lock, which is recorded there too. A state without the record keeps the transition behaviour, a warning. See [updates](updates.md#install-under-a-folder).

### Live settings after an upgrade

A live setting changed with `settings.update` (in the console's Settings, or by command) stays in force when you later run `config update` from your file: Factotum records the change, and the update keeps it (`keptLiveSettings`). Releases dev.82 to dev.88 had a defect here (dev.89, M1): `upgrade apply` recommissions with `config update --current`, and that erased the record of every live change while keeping the values. The first `config update` from your file after such an upgrade then put back the file's value for each of them, silently. From dev.89 the recommission keeps the record, and every `config update` names each live setting whose value it changes (`changedLiveSettings`, with the last live change to it).

The upgrade to dev.89 cannot rebuild a record an earlier upgrade erased. If you upgraded with dev.82 to dev.88 after changing live settings, check before your next `config update`:

1. **What you changed live.** The console's History → Events lists every "Settings updated" event with the settings and values, who changed them and when (History → Commands shows the same `settings.update` commands). The release backups that `upgrade apply` made (`--out`) hold the state as it was before each upgrade.
2. **What is in force now.** `node factory/cli.mjs config show --state <state dir>` (or the console's Settings).
3. **Compare.**
   - A setting whose value now differs from its last live change was reverted by an update after the upgrade. Set it again with `settings.update`; it is then a live change again and later updates keep it.
   - A setting whose value still equals its last live change, but which your configuration file does not have, is at risk: the next `config update` from your file reverts it. Put its value in your file first (setting the same value again with `settings.update` records nothing, since nothing changes). If the update has already run, its result names it under `changedLiveSettings` with that last live change: set it again with `settings.update`.
   - `upkeep.mode` is one of these. Turning it back on (`settings.update {changes: {"upkeep.mode": "observe"}}`, by the operator) pins the upkeep route again.
   - The operator-only settings (`policy.delegatedOperations`, `notifications.handoffAfterDeliveries`, `notifications.handoffRepeatDeliveries`) can only be set again by the operator.

`upgradePreflight(store,{schemaVersions,configVersions,executorDigests=[],requireDrain=true})` checks supported schema/config versions and drain conditions. `stageUpgrade(store,{sourceDir,installRoot,version,compatibility})` records a preparing intent before copying into a new immutable staged version directory and records its digest. An interrupted unpublished copy can be retried only with exactly the same source digest and owned preparing directory; an unowned existing directory is never removed or adopted. The installation root must be outside the source tree, and existing versions cannot be overwritten. `finishUpgrade(store,{version,verify})` rechecks compatibility and code digest, renames the staged directory into an installed version, calls the synchronous local verifier, rechecks that verification did not modify code, then atomically writes `current.json`. Verification failure leaves the previous pointer intact, and an installed-but-unactivated version can be verified again after restart. `resolveInstallation(store,installRoot)` validates the recorded pointer, contained version path, whole-tree digest, supported schema/config, and the explicit `factory/cli.mjs` runtime layout. The CLI `launch --install ROOT --state STATE` starts that exact version's project service and forwards termination signals. Same-version recovery permits unresolved attempts only when every active/stopped flow matches the installed executor digest; selecting a different executor fails closed. Running processes and active source trees are never rewritten. Database migrations and live rolling upgrade are intentionally unsupported; back up and drain first.

## Existing external effects

The CLI `reconcile --state STATE --attempt ATTEMPT_ID --note "Inspection reason"` supports typed read-only reconciliation for an uncertain PR publication, merge, or source-task closure. It requires the original attempt to remain owned and quiescent. The reconciler queries the existing remote object and compares it against retained operation identity, branch/candidate head, authorized merge head, or source revision as appropriate. It never repeats the original write, creates another PR, merges another candidate, or substitutes provider output.

Proven results produce a private reconciliation artifact and durable observation record, then settle through the ordinary engine. The flow remains subject to its stopped state, current authority, and explicit resume controls. Missing or mismatched proof leaves the attempt uncertain. Provider, filesystem, deployment, and unsupported action outcomes still require independent operator inspection; `attempt.resolve` records an explicit failed/not-dispatched disposition with known actual cost and a reason, rather than manufacturing success.

Production network Git operations run within an asynchronous account/repository scope. The configured GitHub account's credential is obtained privately for that invocation and supplied only to the Git subprocess credential helper. The selected remote must identify the configured host/repository; supported SSH URLs are rewritten to that repository's HTTPS endpoint. Repository transport overrides and unexpected credential/HTTP/URL rewriting are rejected. The scope neither changes the user's globally active GitHub account nor forwards its credential to provider subprocesses, durable intents, or logs. Local fictional Git remotes used by injected offline tests do not activate this production network scope.

Concurrent fetches into the project repository are expected: with `policy.autoQueue`, a flow's cleanup and the post-merge `main.verify` start together, and project checks may fetch too. Git locks each ref it updates and checks its expected old value, so the loser of such a race fails on that ref (`cannot lock ref '…': is at … but expected …`, or `Unable to create '….lock': File exists`). A fetch is not atomic across refs: other refs it names may already have been updated or pruned. Repeating it is safe because a fetch is idempotent. Every factory `git fetch` (owned workspaces, source verification, cleanup, `main.verify`, `target.sync`, scheduled tasks, audits and `upgrade apply`) repeats a fetch that failed on such lock contention up to five times with short jittered backoff (about 4 s in all). Other fetch failures are not retried, including ref namespace conflicts (`'refs/remotes/origin/topic' exists; cannot create …`, `try running 'git remote prune'`), even when the same fetch also lost a lock race. If a `.lock` file is still present after the last attempt, the error names it and says it is probably stale: check that no Git process is running in the repository, remove it, and fetch again. There is no cross-process fetch lock, because it could not cover Git processes the factory does not run.

### Cleanup that fails after a completed flow

A delivered flow still completes when its `cleanup` step fails; the flow records `cleanupPending: true` and `cleanupFailure` (attempt, reason, time), and its completion notice says that cleanup is outstanding, naming the workspace, branch and finishing command. The operations loop then retries it after 1, 5 and 30 more minutes. Each retry takes the flow's cleanup lock and runs the same plan as `cleanup plan`, so it refuses whatever that refuses: unsettled attempts, another active flow or ready plan using the workspace, leases, an ownership mismatch, no proven outcome. It carries out only a plan in `release` mode (the same release as the cleanup step) and re-checks other users and ignored files after its fetch, just before removal. A workspace with changed, untracked or ignored files that `cleanup.excludeIgnored` does not cover is never archived or removed automatically: the obligation and notice below are raised at once. A successful retry clears `cleanupPending`, records `workspaceReleased` and `cleanupResolved`, emits `cleanup.retried`, and rewrites the completion notice to say the cleanup finished (event `inbox.updated`). If the last retry fails, it raises the operator obligation `<flowId>:cleanup` and an operator notice `cleanup-notice:<flowId>` (event `cleanup.escalated`). Both name the workspace path, branch, last error and the commands that finish the cleanup: `cleanup plan` and `cleanup apply` (see [workspace cleanup](workspace-cleanup.md)). Finishing it by either path marks the obligation `passed` and supersedes the notice. The loop does not retry while the project is paused or under a recovery hold.

## Integrated feedback and capacity sampling

A maintenance audit returns findings verified against the actual unchanged project input. Stable finding identity includes the normalized source file, source line, message, and digest of the complete current file contents. Engine settlement records one unapproved finding and one stable follow-up obligation. Reobserving the same evidence increments its observation count and preserves an existing resolved disposition; it never creates another attempt-specific follow-up or approves implementation. Findings and rule proposals appear in project snapshots and console detail collections.

Submit a rule challenge through the ordinary canonical command path:

```sh
node factory/cli.mjs command --actor operator --state /private/project-state --operation rule.pushback \
  --payload '{"ruleId":"review.max-rounds","reason":"Explain the observed limitation","evidence":"Reference the retained evidence"}'
```

An optional `flowId` must identify an existing flow. Both the operator and the orchestrator may record proposals under the trusted-account actor model. A proposal never changes policy, approves work, starts a flow, or waives a verification requirement. It remains visible with proposed status until the normal reviewed policy/configuration process handles it.

`machine.sample` is an explicitly authorized maintenance operation, available through manual jobs and durable schedules:

```sh
node factory/cli.mjs maintenance enqueue --actor operator --state /private/project-state \
  --operation machine.sample --note "Record machine capacity aggregates"
```

The Linux sampler reads only load averages, uptime, and total/available memory aggregates from `/proc`. It retains numeric values and observation time, never PIDs, process command lines, usernames, or per-user activity. Engine settlement stores samples by producing attempt, and snapshots expose a bounded recent history. These observations inform capacity diagnosis; they do not independently grant or release the exclusive heavy-work lease.


Completed flows persist a factual retrospective. It records phase durations, explicit question/resource waits, observed findings, repeated step attempts, attributed interventions, known/unknown costs and which acceptance claims remain outside the configured gate. Recording the same projection again is idempotent. `regression.record` adds an operator-attributed later report for a proven merged flow; later red checks alone do not establish causation. Capacity summaries use actual `machine.sample` observations and report sample counts, rather than inferring productive or idle time from wall-clock gaps.


Main verification invalidates previous green health at the serialized dispatch boundary, so a crashed or uncertain worker cannot leave old green authority in force. Infrastructure failures, changed input and target movement produce unknown health; ordinary failing checks, and checks stopped at their time limit, produce red health (see [how long a main verification may run](#how-long-a-main-verification-may-run)). Private `check-N.json` artifacts retain bounded stdout/stderr and each observation. `policy.mainInfrastructureRetries` accepts 0–2, default 0. A retry additionally requires the trusted check's `retrySafe:true` and proof the process never started. Timeouts, cancellation, surviving descendants and unknown executions are never automatically retried. Product failures remain red unless a trusted, unexpired check-specific flake registration explicitly permits their exit code as described below.

Scheduled delivery audits fetch and inspect an owned current-target worktree, recheck the remote head before publishing findings and preserve dirty/divergent work as an operator obligation. Neither audit nor main verification advances the operator's checkout.

### Queue eligibility and idle capacity

Each delivery-mode maintenance tick records the canonical admission reasons for every queued task. The queue row's `eligibility` includes the task revision, first and latest observations, the last reason change, first observed readiness, current ready interval and last blocked observation. Only changed reason sets emit `queue.eligibility-changed` events; repeated ticks update the observation timestamp without adding duplicate transition events. A task revision or replacement queue episode starts a new observation basis.

Successful admission stores `admissionTiming` on the queue row and emits `queue.admission-timing` in the same transaction as the claim. `readyToAdmissionMs` measures from the start of the latest observed ready interval; `firstReadyToAdmissionMs` also includes intervening observed blocked periods. These are elapsed measurements between observations, not proof of continuous eligibility between ticks, productive time or available machine capacity. A direct admission with no prior ready observation has zero observed delay. Diagnostic history does not authorize admission: the canonical checks run again when claiming.

When a commissioned project enables `autoQueue`, has queued work, and has free project change-flow slots for 30 minutes, an operator inbox notice lists the blocked tasks and their reasons. The notice also covers unexpectedly undispatched eligible tasks. It refreshes at most every three hours and uses the same durable transport retry mechanism as other inbox records. A hold, empty queue, full change-flow capacity, disabled automatic queue or withdrawn commissioning resolves the notice and resets its timer. Planning and maintenance flows do not consume change-flow slots. Free project slots do not imply a free shared host-resource permit; inspect machine/resource observations separately.

The queue diagnostics remain in project snapshots, and the durable event collection preserves transitions. These notices request investigation; acknowledging a notification neither approves work nor changes queue eligibility.


### Ranked queues and category capacity

`policy.queueRanking:true` enables ranking of unpinned queued tasks. Higher integer queue priorities run first, then categories in `policy.categoryOrder`, then the operator's existing order. Pins keep their exact operator positions while ranked tasks fill the other slots. With ranking disabled, the operator's order remains authoritative. `policy.categoryLimits` maps category names to positive active-change limits; stopped changes still consume capacity, and a change parked on an answer does not (see [flow slots](#flow-slots-and-parked-flows)). Category limits use the admitted task snapshot, so editing a live task cannot free its category permit.

The operator command `queue.put` accepts optional `entries:[{id,priority,pinned}]` alongside `taskIds`. Priority is an integer between -1000000 and 1000000. Missing metadata is retained across ordinary queue edits. Metadata does not approve a task, bypass a dependency or override conflicts. Ranked order is used consistently by automatic claims, scheduled single dispatches and queue preplanning.

### Weighted host resources

Exclusive `heavy-work` leases remain the default. Configure weighted admission explicitly on every project sharing a coordinator:

```json
{
  "resources": {
    "coordinatorPath": "/private/shared/host.sqlite",
    "pools": {
      "heavy-work": { "capacity": 4, "maxLoadAverage": 8, "minAvailableMemoryBytes": 4294967296 }
    },
    "operations": {
      "gate": { "resource": "heavy-work", "weight": 2 },
      "main.verify": { "resource": "heavy-work", "weight": 4 }
    }
  }
}
```

Weights and capacity are positive integer permits, not estimates of CPU cores or RAM consumption. Acquisition sums all live or uncertain holders transactionally across projects and rejects requests above capacity. A changed shared pool policy is rejected while weighted holders remain; drain them before changing capacity or load thresholds. Machine samples include numeric recorded host-pool capacity, reserved weight and available weight without process identities; dead-but-unreconciled holders remain conservatively reserved in these observations. Optional admission gates read current Linux load and available memory. Missing, stale or invalid measurements deny admission. They gate new work and do not terminate running work when load increases.

Weighted and exclusive requests for the same resource fence one another. A legacy-visible lease remains until every weighted holder is released or proven dead, so an older executor cannot unknowingly take its original exclusive permit while weighted jobs run. Unknown ownership always retains capacity. Gate and main-verification workers keep their leases until supervisor containment drains; commissioning releases its own synchronous lease after the checks finish. Use the same resource name when old and new configurations must serialize; choosing independent pool names deliberately creates independent capacity domains.

### How long a main verification may run

Main checks run one after another in an owned checkout of the target, each under its own `timeoutMs`. The `main.verify` step that runs them is allowed the sum of the `timeoutMs` of the checks it will run, plus the limit of the checkout's `workspace.setup` commands (their count times `workspace.setupTimeoutMs`, 30 minutes each by default), plus 3 minutes for set-up (the heavy-work permit wait, the target fetch and reads, preparing and releasing the checkout), at most one hour. The sum covers the tier being run: a post-merge or refresh verification of the merge tier counts merge-tier checks only, a full verification counts every check. A check with a current flake registration counts once per possible run (`1 + flakePolicy.retries`). For example, checks of 10 and 30 minutes give a 43-minute step. Before dev.85 every `main.verify` step had a fixed 10 minutes, so a check allowed 30 minutes was killed with the step and the flow stopped as an uncertain attempt ("action result missing or altered").

To set the step's limit yourself, add `"timeouts": {"main.verify": <ms>}` (1 to 3,600,000). It replaces the derived limit for every tier. Configuration validation (`config update`, `doctor`) refuses a project whose full set of main checks cannot fit in one step: their `timeoutMs` sum plus the 3 minutes of set-up must be at most one hour, so the checks may add up to at most 57 minutes. Setup commands are not counted against that ceiling, because their limit is usually far above what they take; inside the step the verifier gives each setup command `workspace.setupTimeoutMs` or less, so that setup ends (failing the verification as an infrastructure problem, health unknown, naming the timeout) before the step itself would be ended. A full verification runs every check, of every tier, in one step, so moving a check to the scheduled tier or splitting it into several checks does not make a configuration fit: lower the `timeoutMs` values (each is a ceiling, not the expected run time), or move a slow suite into a scheduled task, which runs in its own step but does not gate main health. It also refuses a `timeouts` entry for a maintenance operation outside 1 ms to one hour.

A check that reaches its `timeoutMs` is stopped and the verification fails: the check's row has `status: "failed"`, `failureClass: "timeout"`, `timedOut: true` and the limit, the remaining checks still run, the target turns red, and the job fails like any red verification (no recovery stop, nothing to `attempt.resolve`). The result carries `timedOut: true`, `timeout: {checkId, timeoutMs, limit, stepTimeoutMs}` and a notice such as `Main check e2e timed out after 30 min (its timeoutMs) and was stopped. Main verification of main at 1a2b3c4d5e6f failed; its health is red until a verification passes.` followed by the last lines of the check's stderr and stdout (at most 12 lines and 1,200 characters each, control sequences stripped, known secrets and credential-shaped text replaced with `[redacted]`, as for a failed trusted command). The failed job's reason and the flow's failure notice ("Failed at run (attempt …): …") carry it. A timeout is never retried automatically. If set-up took longer than its share, or `timeouts["main.verify"]` is shorter than the checks need, the verifier stops a check when the step's time is nearly used up (45 seconds are kept back to release the checkout and record the result) and says so ("was stopped after 2.1 min, before its own timeoutMs of 30 min, because the main.verify step's time was nearly used up"); checks it had no time to start are listed as not run, and the verification fails the same way. Only if the step itself is ended (for example by a hang outside any check) is the attempt uncertain, as before.

**One automatic re-verification after a timeout.** A verification that failed only because checks ran out of time (`timeoutOnly: true` on the target's health) usually met a transient wait, such as a lock held by another job or a slow host. The target stays red, and with `policy.autoQueue` one more verification of the same tier is requested automatically once the usual 15-minute back-off after a failed verification has passed, whether or not work is queued: job `main-timeout-retry:<failed checkedAt>` with `payload.timeoutRetry: true`, event `main.verify-timeout-retry {jobId, check, failedAt}`. The queue status's `main` hold carries `timeoutRetry: {status, after, due, auto, check, jobId}` (`pending`, `requested` or `used`), and its text and the target's health card say where it stands, for example `main health is red: main check e2e timed out; one automatic re-verification is requested after 2026-11-15T10:42:00.000Z`. If the retry passes, the target is green again. The allowance is one retry per target head, whatever the age of the health record: once the retry for a head has run, no further automatic run of that head is started, not even the ordinary refresh of red health older than `policy.mainHealthMaxAgeMs` that queued work would otherwise request. A new head (a merge, a fix) or a `main.verify` the operator enqueues renews it; schedules and factory requests do not. So that a fix pushed outside the factory is noticed, such a target is still probed (one `git ls-remote` at the usual `policy.mainProbeMs` cadence, while work for it is queued or a merge question is open on it, as for green targets): a different remote head is recorded with health unknown (`main.moved`), and that head is then verified as usual. The same head is not verified again automatically. If the retry times out as well (its health then has `timeoutRetry: true`), or a check failed for any other reason, the target stays red and only the operator (`main.verify`, doctor) or a moved target starts another run: `main check e2e timed out again on its one automatic re-verification, so it is not verified again automatically; fix the cause (for example a lock or a slow host), then run main.verify for main`. A later write of the target's health (a merge, a probe, another verification) replaces the record and with it the retry state.

**A check whose own process exited.** The verifier stops waiting for a check 2 seconds after the check's own process exits, even if a process it started (a server or daemon left running) still holds its stdout or stderr open. It stops the processes still in the check's process group and records the check as blocked (`DESCENDANTS_SURVIVE`, health unknown) with its exit code and output, instead of waiting until the check's timeout and reporting a finished check as timed out. Every ending of a check (a timeout, the output limit, processes left behind) empties its process group before the result is recorded: SIGTERM to each member, up to a second for them to exit, SIGKILL to whatever is still in the group, and up to a second more. Each signal goes only to a process that, read from `/proc` just before the signal, is still in the check's process group with the start time it had when found. Known limit: a process that leaves the check's process group (for example with `setsid`, even while being stopped) is not signalled. It is recorded in the check's evidence as `escaped` (PID and its new group), and a timeout that left one behind counts as not contained (blocked, health unknown) rather than a plain timeout (red). Under systemd supervision the step's cgroup check also finds it and the attempt fails as a containment failure; without systemd it keeps running.

Other maintenance steps take their limit from their own bounds; `timeouts["<operation>"]` overrides each:

| Operation | Step limit |
| --- | --- |
| `main.verify` | sum of the checks' `timeoutMs` for the tier, plus 3 minutes, at most one hour |
| `target.sync` | 15 minutes: its Git calls are each bounded at 60 seconds (the fetch is repeated up to six times on ref-lock contention) |
| `audit` | `timeouts.audit` (default 10 minutes) bounds the model call; the step adds 3 minutes for its fetch, checkout and target reads, at most one hour |
| `retention.archive` | the preservation discovery command's `timeoutMs` plus 5 minutes, at least 10 minutes |
| `knowledge.upkeep` | 10 minutes: one fetch of the target when the merge commit is not held yet (repeated on ref-lock contention), then bounded reads of Git objects. No model runs in it |
| scheduled tasks | the task's `timeoutMs` plus 2 minutes (unchanged) |
| others (`maintenance.backup`, `retrospective`, `machine.sample`, `source.*`) | 10 minutes (source adapters are bounded at 5 minutes) |

### Main-check tiers: after every merge or scheduled only

Each `mainChecks` entry may set `tier`: `"merge"` (the default) runs after every merge, `"scheduled"` runs only in a full verification. Projects without the field behave as before: every check is a merge-tier check. At least one check must stay in the merge tier. Split a slow suite out of the post-merge path like this:

```json
"mainChecks": [
  { "id": "unit", "argv": ["npm", "test", "--", "--fast"], "timeoutMs": 600000 },
  { "id": "full-suite", "argv": ["npm", "run", "test:all"], "timeoutMs": 2700000, "tier": "scheduled" }
],
"policy": { "scheduledHealthMaxAgeMs": 93600000 }
```

Then schedule a full verification, for example daily: `schedule.put {schedule: {id: "nightly-main", operation: "main.verify", type: "daily", timeOfDay: "02:00", timezone: "Europe/London"}}`.

What each verification runs (`main.verify` `payload.tier`, `"merge"` or `"all"`, default `"all"`):

| Verification | Runs |
| --- | --- |
| Post-merge baseline (autoQueue) | merge tier (`payload.tier: "merge"`) |
| Admission-triggered refresh (stale, unknown or moved health) | merge tier; every tier when scheduled-tier evidence is missing, expired, or red and main has moved since it failed |
| Configured `main.verify` schedule | every tier, unless its payload says `"merge"` |
| Manual `main.verify` | every tier, unless its payload says `"merge"` |
| Doctor | every tier (it qualifies the whole baseline) |

On a project with scheduled-tier checks, health records the tier it ran (`tier`), the check tiers its evidence covers (`tiers`), each check row's `tier`, and the latest scheduled-tier evidence in `scheduled: {status, head, checkedAt, checks, failed, flowId}`. Projects without scheduled-tier checks store none of these fields. Every write of a target's health (a merge, a dispatch, a probe, target synchronization, a failed settlement, a merge-tier verification) carries `scheduled` forward unchanged; only a full verification replaces it. A blocked full run (any blocked check, an infrastructure error, a moved target or changed input) records no new scheduled evidence, so the previous evidence stands and ages.

Admission requires both tiers:

- Merge tier: green and no older than `policy.mainHealthMaxAgeMs` (one hour), as before.
- Scheduled tier: green and no older than `policy.scheduledHealthMaxAgeMs` (default 26 hours, one hour to seven days, live setting). Missing or expired evidence holds admission; with `autoQueue` the loop requests a full verification.
- A failing scheduled check turns the target red (stored `status: "red"`, with the merge tier's own result in `tierStatus`) and holds admission. A merge-tier pass, a merge, a probe, or a full verification in which another check failed cannot clear it. Only a full verification that passes entirely does: the next scheduled run, a manual `main.verify`, or doctor. With `autoQueue`, the head probe keeps watching a target held by a scheduled-tier red, so a fix merged by the factory or pushed outside it moves the recorded head (the red evidence stays), and a full verification is requested once for each new head, after the usual 15-minute back-off following a failed verification. With `policy.mainProbeMs: 0`, only factory merges are noticed; without `autoQueue`, run `main.verify` after fixing main. The hold text says which applies. A `main.health` operator exception speaks for every tier. Recovery tasks keep their existing bypass.

**A check in both the gate and the scheduled tier.** A check listed in `checks` (the gate) and as a scheduled-tier main check has already passed on every candidate the factory merged, so it breaks on main only through something that skipped the gate, such as a direct push, or two merges that each passed on their own. Until the next full verification records that, main health stays green: unrelated flows keep being admitted, their gates fail on the broken check, and their planners and reviewers may see the breakage and spend plan or review rounds on it. Choose one of these:

- keep the check out of the scheduled tier when it is cheap enough to run after every merge, so a merge-tier verification catches a break;
- keep it only in the scheduled tier, and out of `checks`, when it is too slow for every candidate; or
- if it must be in both, run a full `main.verify` (manual, or `job.enqueue`) after every push to the target that did not come through the factory, and keep such pushes rare.

The queue banner, each task's reasons and the Health card say which tier holds admission and why. Reuse (`reuseMaxAgeMs`), flake registrations, diagnostics, the heavy permit and per-target health work per check in either tier: scheduled rows are reused from `scheduled.checks` only when that evidence is green on the same head. Each target keeps its own scheduled evidence.

### Main-check cadence, evidence and registered flakes

Existing `main.verify` interval or daily schedules control when baseline verification runs. A check may additionally declare `reuseMaxAgeMs` (maximum seven days) to reuse a clean passing result on the exact same target and prepared input. Reuse also requires the same whole project configuration and factory executor, a prior green unchanged baseline, and a preserved artifact matching its recorded digest. Missing or mismatched evidence causes a fresh run. Reuse preserves the original verification time and does not extend its lifetime. Product flakes, infrastructure-retried checks, stale targets and mutated input cannot become reusable clean passes.

A known product flake can be registered explicitly:

```json
{
  "id": "browser-smoke",
  "argv": ["./scripts/browser-smoke"],
  "timeoutMs": 120000,
  "retrySafe": true,
  "flakePolicy": {
    "retries": 1,
    "exitCodes": [1],
    "trackingRef": "quality/browser-smoke-intermittency",
    "expiresAt": "2027-01-01T00:00:00Z"
  }
}
```

A registration requires repeatability, a tracking reference, an expiry and one or two bounded retries for explicit product exit codes. Expired registrations and different exit codes do not retry. A recovered check is marked `flaked:true` and retains every attempt and its output digest in the main-check observation ledger; it is never hidden as an ordinary clean pass. A product failure that does not recover leaves main health red. Infrastructure uncertainty leaves health unknown regardless of a flake registration. Projects must keep their registrations narrow: generic nonzero exit codes can cover unrelated failures, so separate checks or precise exit codes improve discrimination.

## Knowledge upkeep

Optional and off by default (`upkeep.mode`: `off`, `observe` or `propose`). With `upkeep.mode: "observe"` the factory assesses each merged change or document flow as [the design](designs/knowledge-upkeep.md) describes and records what it found. **It writes no candidate and creates no task.** Most runs end at a deterministic pre-filter with no model call. When a declared doc or a governing file refers to what the merge touched, or the flow recorded a verified correction, one small read-only model call follows, and a deterministic validator counts what it would keep. Use it to see how often an assessment is needed and what it would produce. With `upkeep.mode: "propose"` it does the same, and the docs corrections the validator kept become candidates that the factory turns into docs follow-up tasks, unapproved (see [docs follow-ups](#docs-follow-ups-propose)). The recommended path is `observe` until 20 runs are recorded, then `propose`.

**What happens.**

1. When a `forge.merge` result is settled as merged, the same transaction writes a pending receipt for the flow. A flow that later fails or stops at a post-merge step keeps it. Task flows, maintenance flows and flows that did not merge get none.
2. When the flow's retrospective is recorded (the flow finished, or stopped), the receipt becomes a queued `knowledge.upkeep` job.
3. The job runs in its own lane: one upkeep flow at a time, outside the lane other built-in maintenance shares, with no flow slot and no heavy-work permit. A `main.verify` or `target.sync` is never delayed by it.
4. The job reads the merge commit through hash-verified Git objects (fetching the target once if the checkout does not hold the commit yet). If nothing refers to what the merge touched and the flow recorded no verified correction, it ends there.
5. Otherwise it asks the model once, in a throwaway detached checkout of the merge commit (no `workspace.setup`; removed afterwards), and ends with a recorded outcome.

**The model call.** One provider invocation per run at most, read-only, and never repeated: an empty answer is valid, and an answer the validator rejects is counted, not asked again.

- **Model.** The step is `run` of the workflow `maintenance/knowledge.upkeep`. Its role is `providers.upkeep` when the project configures one. Otherwise it is the reviewer's route **as it was when upkeep was turned on**: turning the mode on records that route as the upkeep route, and a later change to `providers.reviewer`, by a delegate or anyone else, does not change the model the assessment runs on. Only the operator changes it: by setting, changing or removing `providers.upkeep` (each pins from the resulting configuration), or by turning upkeep off and on again, which pins the reviewer's route as it is then. Settings shows the pinned route beside the mode. A step model (`stepModels["maintenance/knowledge.upkeep"].run`) or a live default (`models.update`) overrides the role's model for this step, as for any model step. Doctor preflights `providers.upkeep` when it is configured.
- **Bounds.** At most `upkeep.budget.maxTurns` provider turns (6) and `upkeep.budget.timeoutMs` (5 minutes); the evidence it is given is at most `upkeep.budget.maxInputChars` characters (40,000). A route's own smaller turn limit applies. `timeouts["knowledge.upkeep"]`, when set, is the whole step's limit and cuts the call short.
- **What it is given.** A fixed instruction text, then one evidence block: the task's title and acceptance criteria, the merged diff, the lines of each referring file with a few lines either side, the flow's lesson signals, the rules fast checks own, the titles of open candidates and the anchors a capture candidate may name. Every item is labelled with who produced it and, for repository text, its file, line and Git blob id. The instructions never contain evidence, and nothing in the block can close it or pass for an instruction. The prompt is kept with the attempt (`attempts/<id>/provider/prompt.txt`), and what the validator kept and dropped beside it (`upkeep-assessment.json`).
- **The validator.** A docs edit or a governing-file proposal must name a file of the right set and quote, verbatim, a statement in the lines the model was shown. A capture candidate must rest on a verified correction and name an offered anchor and topic. Text the model wrote for later readers is refused when it addresses an agent, claims authority, tells anyone to override other rules or holds a credential; links, commands and mentions of agents are flagged. Whatever fails is dropped and counted under the rule that failed. When a file's excerpts had to be cut to fit, nothing is accepted for that file. Text that holds a character nobody sees (a zero-width space or joiner, a soft hyphen, a bidirectional control, a variation selector, a tag character, a filler) is refused, also in a reason, and the rules are matched on the text with compatibility forms (full-width, mathematical, circled) folded to plain letters. A word whose letters mix scripts, such as a Latin word with a Cyrillic or Greek look-alike letter in it, is refused too, in a replacement, a reason and a quote. Text wholly in one script, in any language, passes, and so does a sentence whose words are each in one script; digits, punctuation and accents belong to no script. A unit written as one word across scripts (`k` with a Greek omega) is refused: write it apart or spell it out.
- **Daily limit.** At most `upkeep.budget.maxModelRunsPerDay` (30) model runs per UTC day. A run reserves its model run before the provider starts, on the UTC day it is at that moment (a step that began before midnight and reserves after it counts against the new day); at the limit it ends `abstained:budget` and is not queued for the next day. A failed, stopped or uncertain attempt stays counted. A model run is given back only when its attempt is settled as not dispatched **and** its receipt names no invocation: a receipt that names one shows that a model ran, so resolving such an attempt as `not-dispatched` does not return the run. An uncertain assessment keeps its reservation until `attempt.resolve` settles it.
- **How a failed call ends.** A model call that ended without a usable result (the provider process exited and its output did not validate), or at its turn limit, fails the attempt: the run closes `model-failed` or `turn-limit`, and there is nothing to reconcile, because the step writes nothing. A call whose end is not known (a timeout, a cancellation, a transport failure, a usage limit) leaves the attempt uncertain, which needs `attempt.resolve` as any uncertain attempt does.
- **Provider usage hold.** While the provider the step would start is held, the job waits in the queue until its run-by deadline. It is never moved to another provider unasked.

**Deadlines.** A receipt not queued within 7 days of its merge is closed (`abstained:no-retrospective`). A run that has not started within `upkeep.budget.maxQueueAgeMs` (24 hours, the most allowed) of first being queued is closed (`abstained:stale-run`). Neither deadline is ever moved later: a configuration change revokes the queued job and the receipt is queued again with the same deadline; a larger configured age changes nothing for a receipt already queued, and a smaller one shortens it.

**Paused admission.** A pause holds the start of every queued maintenance job, knowledge upkeep included: a job queued by hand (`job.enqueue {operation: "knowledge.upkeep", payload: {flowId}}`) or by its receipt waits exactly like a queued `audit`, `main.verify` or backup job, and starts at the first operations tick after `pause {paused: false}`. A job already running when the pause starts finishes. While paused, a merged flow's receipt is still written and queued, and its deadline still runs: a run whose deadline passes during a long pause is closed as `stale-run`, as under a maintenance hold. This is deliberate (dev.89, M6): a pause means that nothing new starts, and an assessment is model work that spends the upkeep budget, so it is held like any other new work rather than made an exception. An audit job seen running during a pause had started before it.

**Drain.** Under a maintenance hold (`pause` with `holdMaintenance`, which `upgrade apply --when-drained` sets) no upkeep job starts and no upkeep run is queued, and a running assessment is asked to stop and then ended by the factory; its receipt returns to pending with the same deadline and is queued again after the hold. One exception: an assessment that had already reserved its model run is closed (`abstained:stopped`, stage `model`) with the run it used, and is not queued again, because an assessment is never retried by itself. A queued or running assessment therefore never holds up a drain. An assessment whose attempt is uncertain is not ended: it needs `attempt.resolve`, as any uncertain attempt does.

**What a run records** (`upkeepRun`, keyed by the source flow id): `outcome` (`nothing`, `abstained`, `invalid` when everything the model returned was dropped, or `candidates` with the number of each kind the validator kept), the `stage` that decided, the `reason`, `flags` (among them what was truncated), counters, the counts the validator dropped by rule (`dropped`), whether a model run was reserved (`modelRun`) and what it used (`usage`). No text: enums and counters only. Stats shows the runs by outcome and the day's totals.

| Reason | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `no-referring-doc` (with the flag `no-signal`) | No declared doc or governing file refers to a touched path, and the flow recorded no signal |
| `weak-signals-only` | No such file, and only weak signals (a plan send-back, an operator note); these never count alone |
| `no-candidate` | The model was asked and reported nothing: the usual answer |
| `insufficient-evidence`, `not-repeatable`, `already-covered` | The model abstained, with its reason |
| `observed` (outcome `candidates`) | The validator kept at least one docs edit, governing-file proposal or capture candidate. In observe mode they are counted and nothing is written |
| `proposed` (outcome `candidates`) | The same in propose mode: the docs candidates are recorded (`recorded` on the run counts them), and the others are only counted |
| `all-dropped` (outcome `invalid`) | Everything the model returned failed a validator rule |
| `budget` | The day's model runs were used up (`maxModelRunsPerDay`) |
| `assessment-unavailable` | Something was to be assessed and no model could be started: every provider the step could start was held. No model ran; this is not a run that reached a model |
| `model-failed`, `turn-limit` | The model call failed, or stopped at its turn limit. The run is closed and not retried |
| `governing-unresolvable`, `learnings-unresolvable` | A `context.files` entry, or `learnings.dir`, cannot be resolved in the merge commit's tree (a link that leaves the repository, a cycle, a submodule), so no file can be shown to be an ordinary doc |
| `suspended`, `backlog-full` | The part that would act is suspended, or the docs backlog is full |
| `no-retrospective`, `stale-run` | A deadline passed |
| `mode-off` | Upkeep was turned off while the run was pending or queued |
| `merge-commit-unavailable`, `error`, `stopped`, `work-limit` | The merge commit could not be read; the assessment failed; it was stopped for a fault; more than 200 runs were in hand |

**Docs and governing files.** The project states its docs in `upkeep.docs.include` (there is no default), less `exclude`. A governing file is never a doc: every `context.files` path, resolved through any symbolic link on the way, is subtracted, and so are upgrade-owned paths and `learnings.dir`. `upkeep.docs.map` (`{docs, paths}`) says which docs describe which source paths. Globs: `*` and `?` stay inside one path segment, `**` crosses segments.

**Settings are authority.** Every field of the `upkeep` and `learnings` blocks, `providers.upkeep` and the step model entry `maintenance/knowledge.upkeep` can be changed only by the operator, on schema 1 and on a migrated project alike, through `config update` or `settings update`. The one exception: anyone who may use `settings.update` (or a delegated `config.update`) may turn `upkeep.mode` down. `upkeep.mode` is a live setting; the rest changes with `config update`. Each limit may be lowered and none raised above its default. The same holds for the live model default of that step: `models.update` for `maintenance/knowledge.upkeep` is the operator's alone, and a delegated `config update --replace-live` cannot discard it. `providers.reviewer` is not upkeep authority and stays as delegable as it was: the assessment does not follow it, because its route is pinned (see the model call above). Settled ceilings: `upkeep.docs.maxFiles` 3, `maxChangedLines` 40, `maxWaiting` 10; `upkeep.context.maxOpen` and `upkeep.capture.maxOpen` 10; `upkeep.budget.maxInputChars` 40,000, `maxTurns` 6, `timeoutMs` 5 minutes, `maxModelRunsPerDay` 30, `maxTasksPerDay` 2, `maxTasksPerWeek` 6, `maxFlowInvocations` 8, `maxOpenTasks` 12, `maxQueueAgeMs` 24 hours; `learnings.maxEntries` 40. Provisional, held as maxima until the release that uses them decides otherwise: `learnings.maxSelected` 5, `maxChars` 6,000. Turning upkeep on is refused while an open task that the factory did not make has an id starting `upkeep:` (in any letter case): finish or close it first.

**In the console.** Settings → Policy → Knowledge upkeep shows the mode and changes it (as the operator). While upkeep is on, the row also names the route the assessment is pinned to and where it came from. For each mode it asks the project what it would answer, judged on the configuration that would result, and offers the modes the project accepts; when none of them is a change, the form says why. So it does not offer `observe` or `propose` while a task the factory did not make is open in the reserved namespace, and does offer `off` on a stored block whose only fault is its mode. (While the record layout has not verified the task kind, the row cannot look for such a task, and the project's own refusal is shown when the change is sent.)

**A block an earlier release stored.** Releases before this one ignored the `upkeep` and `learnings` blocks, so a project may hold values this one refuses (a string where a list belongs, a mode no release has, a limit above its ceiling, an unknown key). Such a project keeps running: upkeep reads as off and every unreadable field as its default. `config update` and `settings update` then say what is wrong, and `upgrade apply` refuses to start wherever it has a verified release directory to check with (always when preparing and with `--merge`; with `--activate` when `--release` is given or the CLI runs from the release artifact; see [updates](updates.md#guided-update-upgrade-apply)). To recover, replace the block with a valid one, for example `"upkeep": {"mode": "off"}`, with `config update`; where the mode is the only fault, setting `upkeep.mode` (in the console, or with `settings update`) is enough. Only the operator can: any change away from such a block is a change of authority. A workflow selector is judged as the catalog compiles it, so `upkeep.docs.workflow` naming a task workflow through its reference is refused like one that says `"kind": "task"`.

**By hand.** `job.enqueue {operation: "knowledge.upkeep", payload: {flowId}, note}` runs upkeep for one merged flow whose retrospective exists (the operator, or the orchestrator where `job.enqueue` is delegated). It keeps the receipt's deadlines and is refused once they have passed. It cannot be scheduled.

**Invocation totals.** While upkeep is on, each attempt settlement adds the attempt's model invocations to the day's `upkeepDay` row (`invocations`, and `upkeepInvocations` for upkeep-attributed attempts: those of the upkeep job's own flows and of every flow whose task carries the factory-written upkeep marker, that is docs follow-ups and context, check, learning and retirement tasks, including the planner, builder and reviewer steps of their flows), and the attempt records that it was counted (`invocationsCounted`). The count is made by the function Stats uses, so the two agree: the invocations a receipt names; else the provider result and its protocol corrections; else one for a model step (`provider.*`, `audit`, `knowledge.upkeep`) that was dispatched and none for one that was not. An upkeep run that ended at its pre-filter counts none. One difference is deliberate: the same recorded result stored under two attempts is counted twice in the day's totals and once in Stats.

**Ceilings and suspension.** There is no target for how much upkeep should produce: nothing is a pass. Each rate below is a ceiling. A count that reaches one suspends a part, with one operator notice, and the part then produces nothing: a run that would ask the model ends `abstained:suspended` at its pre-filter. Nothing polls: a ceiling is judged when a run closes or a disposition is recorded.

| Measure | Window | Ceiling | Suspends |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Runs that reach the model | The last 20 runs | 6 | The model call |
| Runs that produce a candidate | The last 20 runs | 4 | The model call |
| Docs follow-ups that end rejected, failed, declined or expired | The last 10 ended | 3, or 3 in a row | Docs |
| Context or capture candidates declined or expired | The last 10 disposed, per part | 5 | That part |
| A revert or a regression of a change upkeep delivered | The last 20 merged | 1 | Everything |
| Upkeep's share of all model invocations | The last 7 complete UTC days | 10% | The model call and docs |

A window is judged only once it is full, except "3 in a row" and the revert rule. The share reads seven `upkeepDay` rows: yesterday and the six days before. Today is never read, and the window counts as full only when the day totals were being maintained before its oldest day began (counting starts when upkeep is turned on, or at the first evaluation for a project that was already on). A run that ended `assessment-unavailable` is a run, and not one that reached the model. So the two run ceilings cannot suspend anything before 20 runs are recorded, whatever those runs did. The share ceiling is judged by days, not runs: during an observe trial (run `observe` until 20 runs are recorded, then decide), it can suspend the model call before the 20th run once seven complete days have been counted. Review the reason in Health and lift it if the share is acceptable.

**What the share counts, and small projects.** Upkeep's side of the share is not only the assessments. It also counts every model invocation in the follow-up tasks upkeep creates or you accept: the planner, builder and reviewer calls of a docs follow-up, a context task, a check task, a learning task or a retirement task. This is deliberate (knowledge-upkeep design 4.5 and 8.3): those calls are work upkeep caused. The other side is every model invocation of the project. So on a project with little other work, a few follow-ups can reach 10%. In one trial, 20 of 131 invocations over a week were upkeep-attributed (about 15%), and only 8 of those 20 were assessments. Reaching the ceiling suspends the model call and docs follow-ups. That is the expected outcome, not a fault: you review the share and lift the suspension (`upkeep.lift`) if it is acceptable for the project. While upkeep is on, in observe mode as in propose mode, Health shows the share as it stands: upkeep's count and the total for the last seven complete UTC days, the percentage, the 10% ceiling, and, before the window is full, the day it is first judged. It reads the window row and seven `upkeepDay` rows by key. With upkeep off nothing is counted, and Health shows no share line and reads no day row. Stats shows the same totals per day (`upkeep.days[]`).

**A suspension is lifted only by the operator:** `upkeep.lift {part, note}` (`part` is `model`, `docs`, `context`, `capture` or `all`), or Health in the console. It is not delegable, and turning `upkeep.mode` down and up again does not lift it. After a lift, the windows that feed that part are judged only on what is recorded afterwards.

**Findings.** An audit finding and an upkeep candidate are both `finding` rows, and both go through the same states: `unapproved` (open), `tasked`, `delivered`, `failed`, `declined`, `expired`, `resolved-elsewhere`. Each row has a `version`, raised on every change of status and never by being observed again. Three commands act on one, each naming the version that was read:

- `finding.accept {findingId, expectedVersion, task | taskId}` records the task for an **audit finding**: a brief to create (as `task.put` creates it; it still needs approval) or a task that exists. The finding becomes `tasked`. An upkeep candidate is not accepted this way: a docs candidate's follow-up is the factory's to create, governing-context proposals and rules are accepted with `finding.accept {proposals}` (see [Governing-context proposals](#governing-context-proposals-propose)), and check and learning candidates with `finding.accept {findingId, expectedVersion, files | paths, approve?}` (see [Capture candidates](#capture-candidates-propose)).
- `finding.resolve {findingId, expectedVersion, disposition, reason | evidence, note?}` declines it (`reason`: `unnecessary`, `wrong`, `duplicate`, `later`) or records it as `resolved-elsewhere` (with `evidence`). Declining a tasked finding closes its task and declines every finding recorded against that task; stop the task's flow first.
- `finding.reopen {findingId, expectedVersion, note}` reopens a declined, expired, failed or resolved-elsewhere finding. Only a person does: the factory never reopens one, and seeing it again only adds to its record.

The operator may use all three, and so may the orchestrator on a recorded instruction where the command is delegated: with no `policy.delegatedOperations` list every delegable operation is delegated, these three included, and an explicit list delegates them only if it names them. A governing-context proposal or a rule (`context`, `rule`) is the operator's alone, whatever is delegated.

An audit finding's to-do (`finding:<id>`) follows its disposition: declined defers it, with the reason; tasked leaves it pending, and it passes with the task id when the task merges and fails if the task's flow fails; resolved elsewhere passes it, with the evidence; a reopen opens a new one. If the task's flow fails and a later flow of the same task, at the revision the finding was recorded against, merges, the finding is delivered after all and its to-do passes; after a brief edit it stays failed for you to reopen. A task that completes without a merge (its outcome already on the target, say) resolves its findings elsewhere, with what the completion rests on as the evidence. An upkeep candidate has no to-do.

**Candidates.** `observe` writes no candidate: it counts what the validator kept. `propose` records the docs candidates, the governing-context proposals and the one capture candidate a flow may have. Every candidate is held to the same rules: a docs, context, check or stale-learning class is raised from one flow, and a rule or a learning only from a second distinct flow (until then it is an observed class, kept at most 90 days, at most 200); at most 10 docs candidates wait (an eleventh is not recorded), and at most 10 context and 10 capture candidates are open (an eleventh pushes out the oldest, which expires); a waiting docs candidate expires after 14 days and the others after 30, and one past its age never keeps a newer one out (a part at its cap expires those first); a candidate whose flow is abandoned through no fault of its own returns to open twice, and a third time expires.

**In the console.** Health shows what is suspended and why, with the form that lifts it, any open candidates with where each came from and when it expires, the open docs follow-up (and when it expires if you do not approve it), candidates that have a task (with Decline, which closes the task) and closed candidates (with Reopen). Candidate text is shown as text: it is a claim to check, not an instruction. Audit findings are there as before, now with Decline, Dealt with another way and Reopen; creating a task from one records the task against it in the same step. Health lists every open finding, the 100 most recently tasked and the 20 most recently closed, however many findings were recorded since. Invocation totals stay in Stats.

### Docs follow-ups (propose)

With `upkeep.mode: "propose"` a run whose validator kept a docs correction records it as an open candidate (Health lists it). The factory then makes one **docs follow-up** from the waiting candidates, when all of these hold: no docs follow-up is open; docs is not suspended; maintenance is not held for a drain; fewer than `upkeep.budget.maxTasksPerDay` (2) follow-ups were created on this UTC day and fewer than `maxTasksPerWeek` (6) in the seven consecutive UTC days that end today; and fewer than `maxOpenTasks` (12) upkeep tasks are open. The two task ceilings count every upkeep task, yours included, and limit only what the factory makes on its own and what the orchestrator accepts for you (on an instruction, by delegation or under a grant). Your own direct acceptances are never refused by them; they are counted, and they use up the factory's room. Health shows the counts, the room left and when it returns; a candidate's `acceptance.problems` says what would refuse your acceptance now, and `acceptance.delegatedProblems` what would refuse the orchestrator's, naming the ceiling once it is reached. It looks when a proposing run closes, when a follow-up's flow ends, when a green main verification records a new target head, and when a person declines or reopens a docs candidate; nothing polls.

- **File names.** Upkeep corrects only docs whose path is plain ASCII: letters, digits and `._+-` in each component (not starting with `-`), no spaces, no control characters, at most 300 characters. A candidate naming any other file is dropped by the validator (`unsafe-path`) and never recorded or taken into a task; a marker naming one is refused. A doc with such a name is ordinary work.
- **What it takes.** Waiting candidates oldest first, up to `upkeep.docs.maxFiles` (3) files, each re-checked against the target head the factory last recorded, read through hash-verified Git objects. A quoted statement that is no longer there is resolved elsewhere (`statement-gone`). A file that is now governing, excluded, a link or not an ordinary text file is not taken and the candidate keeps waiting until it expires. A head that may not contain the source merge yet (the file differs from what the merge left and the head does not reach the merge commit) leaves the candidate waiting for the next head.
- **The task.** Local, in the reserved namespace (`upkeep:docs:<digest>`), unapproved, ordinary risk, category `knowledge-upkeep`, its paths the declared files. Its title and acceptance criteria are factory sentences that name only the source task, an evidence id and a line number (the file is named in the task's paths and in the evidence, not in a sentence); the quoted statement and the model's reason travel in the task's `upkeep` marker as evidence, and the planner, builder and reviewer get them as a labelled block of data after the acceptance criteria, never as instructions. The marker also holds the declared files with the Git blob each was validated at, and the limits: what the docs check enforces at commit, integration, publication and merge. The marker is part of the brief: it is in the revision, so an approval covers exactly what will be enforced, and on a migrated project it is in the revision's immutable snapshot. Nobody can edit the brief (`task.put` refuses); labels, models and the not-before time still apply.
- **Workflow.** `upkeep.docs.workflow`, when set, is written into the task's `workflow` field and wins over every rule; it must name a catalog definition that is not of kind `task` (refused without a catalog). Without it the first selection rule for the category `knowledge-upkeep` applies, then the default. Commissioning refuses `propose` when that would reach a workflow of kind `task`, or none; an upkeep task is never admitted through a task workflow.
- **Approval.** Explicit, as for any task: the operator; on schema 1 also the orchestrator on a recorded instruction where `task.approve` is delegated; on a migrated project the orchestrator only by citing a grant entry that pins this task id, revision and workflow digest (issue it after the task exists; Tasks shows the pins). The factory never approves one. A follow-up not approved within 14 days expires its candidates and is closed.
- **Its flow.** Admitted only while a parked place is free (`policy.maxParkedFlows`), and counted last among parked flows. With parking off (`policy.maxParkedFlows: 0`) no follow-up is ever admitted, and the refusal says so: a follow-up would otherwise hold a flow slot while it waits on its merge decision. Before its first model step the factory checks that each declared file is still exactly the version it was validated at, at the base its workspace was prepared from (otherwise it ends as `stale`) and at the target head it last recorded (otherwise `base-moved`). Its merge question goes to the merge decision role and is never answered by `policy.autoMerge`. The factory ends the flow, as `flow.abandon` would and only when no attempt is in hand and no merge is proven, when it reaches any question other than its merge decision, when it has used `maxFlowInvocations` (8) model invocations, when it is stopped (a merge question that expired, for example), when it is parked beyond the allowance, or under a drain hold (a parked one at once, a working one stopped with an abort and ended once its attempt has settled). Ended under a drain, as `stale` or as `base-moved` (also when its commit, integration, publication or merge was refused only because a declared file is no longer the pinned version, or its integration conflicted, and it then reached a question or its graph's failed end), its candidates return to waiting (a third return expires them); otherwise they are recorded as failed, for you to reopen. The task is closed, the time it held a slot without working is recorded, and one notice says what happened. A flow whose attempt is uncertain is never ended: one notice asks for `attempt.resolve`. Its workspace is kept for the usual failed-flow cleanup.
- **Its pull request.** When the factory ends a follow-up that published a pull request (or its graph ends failed after publishing), it closes that pull request with a comment, through a maintenance job (`forge.closeFollowUp`) run outside the transaction like any forge effect; an uncertain outcome is reconciled with `attempt.resolve` as usual, and running it again is harmless (a closed pull request is left as it is, and the comment is written once). A job that is revoked, fails or is abandoned is queued again, at most three times; then, or when the forge shows a pull request the factory must not close, an operator to-do (`upkeep-pr-close:<flow>`) says so. Only the pull request the factory itself opened for that follow-up is closed: the factory records, when it creates a pull request, its number, its author (the configured `repository.account`, whose token every call uses) and its head repository, and the close checks all of them, the factory's creation marker for that flow, and the head and branch it pushed; a merged, moved, forked or foreign one is left alone and an operator to-do says so. A pull request the factory has no record of opening itself (one adopted through the publication lookup, or published before this was recorded) is never closed automatically. Before any write the close reads the pull request's timeline: once the factory has closed it, it never closes it again, so a pull request a person reopened after the factory closed it is left open and settled as `reopened`, with an operator to-do.
- **Declining** a tasked candidate (Health, or `finding.resolve`) closes the follow-up and declines every candidate on it.

### Governing-context proposals (propose)

With `upkeep.mode: "propose"` a run whose validator kept a proposal against a governing file records it as an open candidate: the file (as its configured `context.files` entry reaches it), the quoted statement, a replacement of at most 600 characters, the model's one-line reason, the file's Git blob at the merge commit, and the context entries that reach the file. It is never an edit, and only you can accept it.

- **Text rules.** The replacement and the reason (and, for docs and capture candidates, their reasons too) must pass the text rules, look-alike letters and split words included; a candidate that fails is dropped before it is recorded. Health withholds any stored text that fails them.
- **The waiting notice.** One operator notice says how many proposals are waiting, once three are open or the oldest has waited seven days. It is judged when upkeep records proposals and when someone acts on one (nothing polls), and it is resolved when none is waiting.
- **Accepting.** Health shows each proposal with the entries it names and, for those that can be accepted, **Accept this proposal** (and **Accept N proposals as one task** when several can). Before accepting, Health says how many unfinished flows have a reviewed plan: once the change merges, such a flow replans if it needs another build round after taking in the new target. The command is `finding.accept {proposals: [{findingId, expectedVersion, digest}]}`: each proposal's version and the digest of what was shown (Health shows both). It is yours alone, acting directly: it is refused to the orchestrator on any instruction, by any delegation and under any grant. In one transaction it checks every proposal (open, at that version, its stored file, quote, replacement, reason, file version and entries matching the digest), writes **one** task of kind `context` from the stored proposals (`upkeep:context:<digest>`; its title and criteria are factory sentences naming evidence ids only; the quoted statements, exact replacements and reasons travel in its marker as evidence), names the context entries in `governingEdits`, approves that revision as your own approval (the same record `task.approve` makes for a governing edit), and marks the proposals tasked. Any refusal writes nothing.
- **What can be accepted.** Every context entry that reaches the proposal's file must be configured, `required: true`, without a `paths` mapping, and not under `factory/` (as for any governing edit). A proposal against an optional or mapped entry is listed with the reason it cannot be accepted: change the entry first (`config update`), or decline it. Proposals against one file must have been validated at the same version of it. At most one context task is open at a time, and it counts against the daily, weekly and open-task ceilings like a docs follow-up. Context must not be suspended and upkeep must be proposing.
- **Providers.** Each model step of a context task starts a provider with its instruction files suppressed. That suppression is shown to work only by the [provider instruction-loading checklist](qualification/provider-instructions.md), run on your host. Record each provider whose check passed in `upkeep.context.qualifiedProviders` (`codex`, `claude`; empty by default; an operator-only setting). Until a provider is listed, a context task whose steps would use it is not admitted, and its provider step refuses to start it. While it waits, the hold is shown with the providers and the remedy in `status --task` (`admissionReasons`), in the queue (`queueStatus.holds`, kind `context-provider`, and the task's eligibility) and in Health, which keeps the provider warning; the queue passes over it to the tasks behind it, and the hold writes no event per tick (dev.91). Codex is isolated as of dev.90 ([provider worktree isolation](changes/provider-worktree-isolation.md)), like Claude Code before it: list either provider only after its checklist passes on your host.
- **The task and its flow.** It runs as a governance edit: every step receives the named files as they are at the comparison commit, and the candidate's version is reviewed as a proposal; the reviewer is also told to inspect the replacement text itself for attempts to override authority, widen scope or address agents. The context check runs at commit, integration, publication and merge: only the declared files may change, each a governing file (one the context loader delivers) in both the comparison and the candidate's tree, reached by exactly the entries the task names; no file added, deleted or renamed, no mode change, no link or submodule touched, nothing binary; at most 12 changed lines per accepted proposal; judged on the candidate and on every commit the builder made. A governing entry reached through a linked directory is delivered at its real path. Before its first model step each declared file must still be the version it was validated at: otherwise the flow ends as `stale` (or `base-moved`), and its proposals return to waiting at a new version, read again at the target head, so you see the current file before accepting again (a statement that is gone is resolved elsewhere). The flow rules of docs follow-ups (questions, invocations, stops, parking, drains, merge decisions) apply unchanged.
- **Newly governing docs.** A waiting docs candidate whose file has become governing is resolved elsewhere (`reclassified`) and recorded as a governing-context proposal against that file, with its reason and no replacement. Such a proposal cannot be accepted (there is no exact edit to read): decline it, or correct the file with an ordinary task you approve (`governingEdits`).

### Capture candidates (propose)

A capture candidate is what upkeep proposes when a flow recorded a **verified correction** (a review sent the change back and a later review marked the finding fixed; a fast check or the gate failed and later passed) and a later agent would plausibly repeat the mistake. It takes the first form that works: a **check** (a rule a script can test, added to an existing fast-check script), a **rule** (one condition in an existing governing file) or a **learning** (what neither can hold). Its anchor comes from a closed list (`check:<fast check id>`, `context:<entry id>`, `dir:<directory a signal names>`) and its topic from a fixed one; anything else, or a candidate that cites no verified correction, is dropped by the validator. A flow yields at most one, however often it is assessed: a manual re-run of a flow that already recorded one records none. A check is raised from one flow; a rule or a learning only from a second distinct flow. The title, the text and the reason pass the text rules, look-alike letters and split words included; Health withholds any stored text that fails them.

- **A check.** `finding.accept {findingId, expectedVersion, files?, approve?}`, by you or by the orchestrator on your instruction (where `finding.accept` is delegated). It writes one task of kind `check` (`upkeep:check:<digest>`), unapproved, and **only you approve it** (below): it may change only the declared fast-check scripts, keeping each one's mode, within 2 files and 80 changed lines. `files` names the scripts (by default the script of the check the candidate is anchored to). Only a configured fast check's **script** can be declared, identified from its `argv` by exactly one of two rules: `argv[0]` is a repository path (it contains `/`, is not absolute, has no `..`), or `argv[0]` is one of `node`, `deno`, `bun`, `python`, `python3`, `bash`, `sh` and the script is `argv[1]` (`argv[2]` for `deno run` and `bun run`), not starting with `-`. No argument after the script is ever eligible, so in `["python", "checks/verify.py", "src/schema.json"]` only `checks/verify.py` is. Every other shape offers nothing: an option before the script, inline code (`sh -c`, `python -c`, `python -m`), a wrapper (`npm`, `make`, `env`), an absolute or versioned interpreter. The script must also be a tracked ordinary or executable file named exactly (no link on its path), within the filename policy, in no hidden folder and with no hidden name (so never provider configuration such as `.codex/`, `.agents/`, `.claude/` or `.mcp.json`, nor `.github/`), not governing, not upgrade-owned, not in the learnings directory, and not named by any other word of the configuration: no argument of a fast check other than its own script position, and no string in any other setting (gate and main checks, workspace setup, preview, sources), whole or as a token of a longer string, reaches the same file once resolved in the tree, normalization (`checks/./verify.mjs`, `src/../checks/verify.mjs`) and links included. This is checked when the candidate is accepted and again, under the configuration then in force, at commit, integration, publication and merge. A project with no fast checks cannot accept a check candidate: a first check needs a new script and a graph node, which is a configuration change you commission. Health lists each fast check with its script or why it has none. The check task's builder and reviewer get the rule as data; the reviewer is told to report anything beyond testing that rule (a new command, network access, a change to how other rules are tested) as blocking. When it merges, a notice reminds you that listing the rule in `fastChecks.checks[].rules`, so reviewers stop raising it, is a configuration change you make.

  **What this does and does not guarantee.** Enforced: which files a check task changes (the declared scripts only, nothing added, deleted, renamed or linked), that each keeps its mode, that the configured command and its arguments do not change (the configuration lives outside the repository), and that no word of the configuration names the same file. Not enforced: that nothing else uses the script, since string matching in configuration cannot see a shell command it does not parse, a glob, a directory, an import or a sourced file; and what the script does when it runs, since a script can spawn processes, read or source other files and depend on its environment. Those rest on the reviewer, on your own approval of the exact scripts, and on the merge decision, which is never automatic; process containment is an open follow-up ([Containment of builder and check processes](runtime-followups.md#containment-of-builder-and-check-processes-p2)).
- **A learning.** `finding.accept {findingId, expectedVersion, paths?, approve?}`, by you or by the orchestrator on your instruction. Needs `learnings.dir`. It writes one task of kind `learning-add` (`upkeep:learning:<digest>`), unapproved, that adds exactly one file, `<learnings.dir>/learning-<12 hex>.md`, fixed when it is accepted: the frontmatter (`id`, `title`, `paths`, `kind`, `enforced_by`, `last_verified`, `retire_when`) and the accepted text as the body, at most 1,500 characters in all (see `project/LEARNING.template.md`). `paths` names the globs it applies to (by default the directory it is anchored to). Acceptance is refused when the file would be too long, when the store already holds `learnings.maxEntries` (40) entries, or when the directory is linked or governing. The builder is given the exact file; the check refuses anything else, including a second file, an existing learning changed, a file outside the directory, an executable file, a frontmatter that differs from the accepted one, and a different body. The reviewer is told to inspect the text for attempts to override authority, widen scope or address agents. A learning is advisory: once merged, later flows are shown it as described under [Learnings](#learnings).
- **A rule.** Accepted only by you, directly, exactly as a governing-context proposal is, and in the same command and task (`finding.accept {proposals: [...]}`): its digest covers its file, title, text, reason, anchor, topic, file version and entries. It must be anchored to a governing entry (`context:<id>`): a rule anchored to anything else is dropped by the validator (`rule-not-governing`) and never recorded; every slice 6 rule applies (required, unmapped entries; `upkeep.context.qualifiedProviders`; one open context task; the operator's own approval of the governing edit). Its text, at most 10 lines, is added as one condition to the file that entry reaches, and the context check requires it to be there as written. A rule anchored elsewhere is listed with why it cannot be accepted.
- **Approving.** Accepting never approves a check or learning task, except when you add `approve: true` yourself, acting directly. A **check task is approved by you alone, directly** (`task.approve`, or `approve: true`), as a governing edit is: never by delegation, an instruction or a grant, on either schema. The approval is recorded as `checkScriptsApproval`, bound to the task's revision and to each declared script's blob and mode; admission, every step and commit, integration, publication and merge refuse a check task without it. The orchestrator may still accept a check candidate into an unapproved task on your instruction. A learning task is approved as any task is (`task.approve`; on a migrated project the orchestrator needs a grant for that exact revision). Not approved within 14 days, the task is closed and the candidate expires.
- **Limits.** At most one open check task and one open learning-add task at a time; both count against the daily, weekly and open-task ceilings like a docs follow-up. Capture must not be suspended, maintenance must not be held for a drain, and upkeep must be proposing. The flow rules of docs follow-ups apply unchanged; before its first model step each declared script must still be the version and mode it was offered at, and the learning file must still not exist.

### Learnings

`learnings.dir` names one project-owned directory of learning files, `learning-<12 hex>.md`, each at most 1,500 characters with its frontmatter and title (see `project/LEARNING.template.md`). A file is read only in that exact layout: the seven fields one per line in the template's order, the title as one JSON string, the paths as a JSON list of quoted globs (spaces after the commas or inside the brackets are fine, as in `paths: ["src/a/**", "test/a.test.mjs"]`), a blank line, the body and one final newline. A file the factory adds for an accepted learning is written exactly as accepted, with no spaces in its list. A refused file is listed in Health with the line that differs and what it should read. The `learnings` block is independent of `upkeep`: a project with a learnings directory and upkeep off still has its learnings delivered. Its settings are upkeep authority (operator only): `dir`, `maxSelected` (at most 5), `maxChars` (at most 6,000) and `maxEntries` (at most 40); each may be lowered, never raised. The directory may not have a hidden folder on its path (`.claude/`, `.agents/`, `.codex/`, `.github/` and the like are where providers look for instructions, skills and settings), and may not hold a governing-context path or lie inside one; both are refused when the configuration is written.

**What a flow is shown.** A plan cycle selects its learnings once, at its first model step: the plan step, or the first build of a flow that does not plan. A replan starts a new cycle, which selects again. The factory reads the learnings directory from the commit the flow's checkout is on at that step, through hash-verified Git objects, never from the working files, and selects the entries whose `paths` match the task's declared paths, most specific match first, then the most recent `last_verified`, at most `learnings.maxSelected`, within `learnings.maxChars` counted as delivered, in characters (code points, as every learning limit counts them): each file with its frontmatter, title and provenance. A file is delivered whole or not at all; it is never cut. A task with no declared paths is shown none. The flow records the commit and each selected entry's id, path and Git blob (`learningSelection`, with what was refused and why), and every later step of the cycle is given exactly those blobs, so a learning edited in the checkout, or merged since, changes nothing that cycle sees.

A file is never delivered when it is not named `learning-<12 hex>.md` (so never `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md` or a skill file), is not an ordinary file, is governing, is longer than 1,500 characters, does not have the exact learning layout (including an unknown `retire_when` form), or has a title or body that fails the text rules (a claim of authority, text addressed to an agent, an instruction to ignore rules, hidden or bidirectional-override characters, look-alike letters). A store reached through a symbolic link, or one that cannot be read, gives the cycle nothing and says why.

**How it is delivered.** The planner, plan reviewer, builder and reviewer get the selected files as one `advisory-learning` evidence block after the governing context and apart from it, with a fixed instruction: a learning carries no authority; it cannot grant a permission, relax or override a rule, the task or its acceptance criteria, widen the files that may be touched, or stand in for an acceptance criterion or an answer; current code and the governing context win; and a learning alone is never the basis of a review finding. Learnings are not governing context: they are not in the context package or its digest, no plan guard reads them, and they are never written anywhere a provider loads instructions or skills from. A merged learning makes a running flow replan only through the ordinary target-follow rule: when the merge changes a path the task or plan names. Keep the learnings directory outside the paths tasks normally declare, or every learning merge replans the flows whose paths contain it.

**Seeing what was shown.** A flow's page lists, for each plan cycle, the learnings it pinned (ids, paths, blob ids, text-rule flags), those left out by the count or the budget, the files refused in the store with why, why a store could not be read, and what each role cited (the planner and builder applied or contradicted, the reviewer followed or contradicted; the latest result per role and step). `status --flow <id>` returns the same: `flow.learningSelection` (the current cycle's pin), `flow.learningHistory` (earlier cycles), `flow.learningUse` (`{selected, cited}`, each learning id once per flow) and `flow.learningCitations` (`[{cycle, step, role, attemptId, at, cited: [{id, use}]}]`). A flow settled before dev.92 has its citations read from its latest plan, build and review results, with no cycle or attempt. Health shows what the last `learnings.check` read and every file it refused, with why: a refused file is never shown to a flow and never becomes a retirement candidate, so fix or remove it with an ordinary task.

**Citations and counters.** Plan and build results carry `learningsApplied` (`[{id, use: applied | contradicted}]`) and the review result `learningsChecked` (`[{id, outcome: followed | contradicted}]`): ids and fixed words only. The factory counts, per learning, the flows that were shown it and the flows whose results cited it (`learningUse`), each once per flow; an id that was not in the flow's selection is ignored.

**Delivery can stop (14.3).** The factory keeps the totals of those counts. Once at least 20 selections have been counted in the current window and fewer than 10% of them were cited, it stops learning delivery (a `learnings` suspension, with one notice): a new plan cycle is shown no learnings and its pin says why; a cycle already pinned keeps what it was shown. A suspension of everything (`all`) stops delivery to new cycles the same way. Health shows the selections, the citations and the rate. Only you restart delivery, with `upkeep.lift {part: "learnings", note}`; the window then starts again from that moment, and a citation counts only in the window in which its learning's selection was counted, so a flow that was shown a learning before the lift and cites it afterwards does not count in the new window.

**Retiring a learning.** `learnings.check` is a read-only maintenance job (enqueue it with `maintenance enqueue --operation learnings.check`, or schedule it). It reads the learnings directory at the target head the factory last recorded and judges six typed conditions; it calls no model and deletes nothing.

| Condition | Fires when |
| --- | --- |
| `paths` | The entry's `paths` match no file at the target head |
| `enforcement` | `enforced_by` names a fast check or a context entry that is no longer configured |
| `unused` | Shown to at least ten flows and never cited |
| `retire_when: path-absent:<glob>` | No file matches the glob |
| `retire_when: file-present:<path>` | The file exists |
| `retire_when: check-present:<id>` | A fast check with that id is configured |

`retire_when: none` relies on the built-in three. In `propose`, each condition that fires raises one `learning-stale` candidate (one per learning and condition; the same pair is never raised twice), and an open one is resolved elsewhere at a later check once its learning is gone (`learning-gone`) or its condition no longer fires (`condition-cleared`), and so is a tasked one whose retire task no flow has started yet (its task is closed). When a later check reads a learning differently while its condition still fires, an open candidate takes the current file and blob at a new version (read it again before you accept), and one the factory had resolved is raised again: a new breakage is always actionable. A candidate a person declined stays declined; reopening it (`finding.reopen`) takes what the latest check read. In any other mode the job reports what fires and writes nothing. A walk of the tree is bounded; when it is cut short, the conditions it could not judge do not fire and nothing is resolved.

`finding.accept {findingId, expectedVersion, approve?}`, by you or by the orchestrator on your instruction, writes one task of kind `learning-retire` (`upkeep:retire:<digest>`), unapproved. It is refused unless the learning file at the target head is still exactly the content `learnings.check` read (a learning repaired or reworded since is a different learning, which the next check judges afresh) and the candidate's condition still fires there, judged with the current counters. The task is pinned to that content: it may only delete that one file, with exactly that content. Before its first model step the flow checks the file and the condition again; if either changed it ends as stale, the candidate returns, and the next check resolves it. The check refuses, at commit, integration, publication and merge, an edit to the file (even in a builder's own commit that a later one deletes), a deletion of any other file, an addition, and a file whose content changed since acceptance. It is approved as a learning-add task is, and one learning-retire task is open at a time. Correcting a learning in place is an ordinary task. At the store's cap, a new learning can be accepted only after a retirement has merged.

**Cost when idle.** A tick with no upkeep work reads one meta row for upkeep and starts no process. Learnings add nothing to the tick: selection runs inside a model step's preparation, and `learnings.check` only when it is enqueued or scheduled.

## Issue source synchronization

`source.sync` is an allowlisted maintenance job and schedule operation. The Tasks view can enqueue it with **Refresh from source**; Settings can schedule it. It imports without approval, revokes approval on changed briefs or dependency facts, and records observation and scan status. See [issue source sync](source-sync.md) for eligibility, bounded GitHub discovery, the custom adapter contract, and scheduling examples.

## Maintenance attention notices

Routine successful built-in maintenance stays in History rather than creating an inbox notice. This applies to scheduled and manually enqueued jobs. Passing main verification, backups, machine samples, audits without findings, routine retrospectives, knowledge-upkeep assessments and successful issue lifecycle publications are quiet when their results contain no blocked/failing route, findings or preservation obligations.

A `source.sync` completion is quiet only when its retained report is complete, successful, error-free and has zero imported, changed and revoked tasks. Source changes, partial scans and missing or unknown result evidence still produce an attention notice. Failed and stopped maintenance remains visible. Development flow and custom integration/release completion notices keep their existing behavior. Job, flow, event, attempt evidence and retrospective retention is unchanged.

Quiet jobs create no inbox item, so they produce neither an attention webhook nor a subsequent resolution webhook. Existing notices are preserved across upgrades; acknowledge them normally. Resolution notifications remain enabled for real operator questions and notices.
